A safety-concern note that captures the hazard, the location, the people at risk, and the escalation, so the report reaches someone who can act. The entry should be dated and signed by the agent who handled it. Keep the supporting note with the call record so a reviewer can verify it. The team lead should review the entry at the next shift meeting.

## Key takeaways

- Record the request in the caller's words before explaining the limit.
- Name the owner, the time, and the next step.
- Use the approved verification or secure channel.
- Review the record for patterns, not for individual blame.
- Close the loop with the caller in writing where the policy allows.

## Capture the hazard and the location

Record what happened or what the caller saw, where it happened, and when. A note that says safety issue cannot be routed or investigated. Record the caller's words and the exact location. The template should prompt the facts before the opinion.

Record the people at risk and whether anyone was injured. A hazard near a public entrance is different from one in a closed storage room. Record the exposure and the time. The template should help the team prioritize.

## Separate the report from the remedy

Record what the caller wants, such as an inspection or a repair, without promising the outcome. A promise made at intake may not match the site's authority. Record the request and the person who will decide. The template should keep the response honest.

Record any immediate action the caller was told to take, such as avoiding an area or calling emergency services. A safety call may need an immediate instruction. Record the advice and the time. The template should not delay a real emergency.

## Route the note to a named owner

Name the person or team that will receive the concern and the expected response time. A note sent to a general inbox may sit for days. Record the owner and the time. The template should show the route, not only the report.

Record the acknowledgment sent to the caller and the next update date. A caller who reports a hazard and hears nothing may escalate publicly. Record the message and the date. The template should close the loop.

## Review safety reports for patterns

Review the reports by location, type, and time. A cluster at one site may point to a maintenance or staffing problem. Record the pattern and one action. The review should not identify the caller without consent.

Check that every report has an owner, a response, and an outcome. A report with no owner is the finding. Record the gap and the fix. The next review should show a complete record.

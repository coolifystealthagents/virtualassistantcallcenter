## Route the reason before the details

Orthodontic calls can be new-patient consultations, adjustment questions, appointment changes, records requests, or billing matters. Ask what the caller needs first. The route should also account for whether the caller is an existing patient, parent, guardian, or referring office under the practice’s approved identity rules.

## Make consultation requests complete

For a new consultation, collect caller name, callback number, patient age range, preferred location, and acceptable times. Ask only approved intake questions. Avoid making clinical claims, estimating treatment, or implying insurance approval before the practice reviews the request.

## Handle existing-patient messages carefully

Record the patient reference through the approved system and write the exact question. A broken appliance or discomfort concern should follow the practice’s stated clinical escalation path. Do not turn a message into a diagnosis. For routine changes, confirm the requested date and the next confirmation step.

## Review the handoff

Sample calls for privacy, correct routing, understandable scheduling language, and complete notes. The office should be able to see what happened, what the family needs, and who is responsible next.

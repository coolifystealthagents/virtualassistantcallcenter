## Route by project stage

Remodeling callers need different help before an estimate, during design, while work is active, and after handoff. Ask whether there is an existing project and capture its address or reference. A new kitchen inquiry should not enter the same queue as a change request from an active job.

## Capture the decision details

Record name, callback number, property address, project reference, request, desired timing, and access constraints. Note the change or concern in the caller’s own words. Do not approve scope changes, make schedule promises, or interpret contract terms without the responsible owner.

## Make the handoff accountable

Include what has already happened, what the caller wants, and the next response needed. Assign the message to a project or estimating owner with a due time. If the owner is unavailable, explain the fallback accurately and keep the record visible.

## Learn from project-call friction

Review calls that bounced between estimating, design, and field staff. Missing addresses and unclear project references usually point to an intake gap. Fix that gap so the caller reaches the right person with less repetition.

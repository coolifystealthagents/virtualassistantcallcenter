## Name the bookable appointment types

Create a booking worksheet beside the approved calendar. It should list appointment type, duration, eligible staff or resource, location, lead time, buffer, required intake, confirmation method, and who may override constraints.

## Publish a scheduling worksheet

Capture caller name, callback number, email if offered, appointment type, purpose at the needed level, location, preferred dates, timezone, staff preference, required resource, accessibility request, chosen slot, confirmation status, change policy acknowledged, and booking owner.

## Match need before offering time

Read back date, local time, location or channel, and next requirement. The [appointment setting service](/services/appointment-setting) can support this flow, but the business remains responsible for its availability and booking rules.

## Limit calendar permissions

A scheduler may offer visible eligible slots and send approved confirmations. They may not double-book, shorten required duration, promise a specific outcome, waive deposits or policies, or place a caller into a restricted appointment type.

## Resolve conflicts without improvising

Calendar conflicts, unavailable required resources, urgent requests, accessibility needs, and policy disputes go into a scheduling exception queue with an owner and response time. Preserve the requested windows rather than guessing a slot.

## Pilot the booking conversation

Book a standard visit, a caller in another timezone, a request needing special equipment, and a slot that disappears during the call. The caller should always hear the actual status before disconnecting.

## Check booking integrity

Review complete bookings, calendar conflicts, confirmation delivery, reschedule and cancellation requests, unconfirmed holds, exception age, and corrections after booking. Track outcomes by appointment type because rules differ.

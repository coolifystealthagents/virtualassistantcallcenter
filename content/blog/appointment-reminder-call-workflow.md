## Decide what a reminder may accomplish

Use a daily reminder roster linked to the source schedule. Each row should offer only approved outcomes: confirmed, reschedule requested, cancellation requested, voicemail left, no answer, wrong number, or staff review.

## Prepare the reminder roster

Include appointment ID, date and local time, location, approved contact name, phone number, communication permission, reminder window, identity check result, outcome, requested change, message type, attempt time, staff owner, and roster sync time.

## Protect privacy in messages

The final step is reconciliation. A spoken confirmation that never reaches the scheduling system creates false confidence for both the business and the customer.

## Control changes and exceptions

The caller may confirm an existing appointment and, when authorized, use approved rescheduling slots. They should not reveal sensitive appointment details in voicemail, change service type, waive fees, interpret preparation instructions, or create an unapproved slot.

## Close the loop in the schedule

Wrong numbers and stop requests suppress further reminders through the approved process. Conflicting schedule data, accessibility requests, late cancellation disputes, and questions requiring professional judgment go to staff without changing the source appointment.

## Test common reminder outcomes

Try a confirmed appointment, a generic voicemail, a same-day change request, and a caller who says the time is wrong. Verify that the schedule and reminder roster end in the same state.

## Measure contact without overstating impact

Track eligible appointments, attempts, verified contacts, confirmations, change requests completed, wrong numbers, opt-outs, unresolved exceptions, and roster reconciliation errors. No-show rate may be observed, but reminders alone should not be assumed to cause every change.

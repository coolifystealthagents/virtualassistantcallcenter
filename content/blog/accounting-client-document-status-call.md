## Verify before discussing the account

A caller asking whether documents arrived may sound routine, but the answer can reveal a client relationship and private financial activity. Use the firm's approved identity check before reading any account status. If the caller is an employee, spouse, or other representative, confirm that the record permits access.

Never ask for passwords, full account numbers, or a complete taxpayer identification number. When verification fails, record the callback request and send it to the firm's authorized contact.

## Report only the status shown

Use the exact status available in the approved system, such as upload received, review pending, or staff question sent. Do not say a return is complete, accepted, or ready to file unless authorized staff recorded that state. Avoid interpreting a missing-document list or advising the client what a tax form means.

If the caller says they sent a file that is not visible, note the filename, channel, and approximate sending time. Do not ask them to resend sensitive files through ordinary email unless that is the firm's approved method.

## Route the unresolved question

Create one callback item with the verified client, question, best contact method, and any stated deadline. Route it to the account owner or seasonal backup listed in the directory. The assistant should not choose a preparer based on who appears online.

Close the task only when the owner responds or takes control. During busy season, review aging status calls each day. A rising backlog may point to unclear portal messages or an outdated ownership table rather than poor phone handling.

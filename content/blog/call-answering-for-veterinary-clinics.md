## Start with the animal and reason

Ask which animal needs help, why the caller is contacting the clinic, and whether the animal is an established patient when that matters to the next step. Keep the question neutral. Do not offer a diagnosis or treatment recommendation.

## Recognize urgent concerns

Use the clinic’s approved emergency language and escalation path for reports of breathing difficulty, serious injury, poisoning, or another listed urgent sign. Record the caller’s words and location. Do not create a delay by collecting details that the urgent owner does not need.

## Handle routine requests carefully

For appointments, refills, records, and preventive questions, capture the requested service, preferred timing, callback number, and owner. Send clinical questions to the authorized team. Only share information that the clinic has approved for the phone path.

## Review handoff quality

Check whether notes distinguish urgent from routine calls and whether the receiving owner is clear. Repeated confusion usually points to an opening question or escalation rule that needs revision. Keep the record factual and limited to the next decision.

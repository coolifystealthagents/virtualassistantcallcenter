## Identify the referral pathway

Home-health calls can come from a family member, discharge team, clinician, payer, or prospective client. Ask who is calling and what service or next step is requested. Keep referral intake separate from existing-client scheduling and clinical questions.

## Capture the minimum referral record

Use the approved process for caller identity, callback number, referral source, patient context, requested service, location, and documentation status. Record facts without interpreting eligibility or care needs. Sensitive information belongs only in the authorized system.

## Route clinical decisions appropriately

Questions about care, safety, eligibility, or urgency must reach the qualified owner through the organization’s approved path. Administrative requests can receive a named callback and due time. State what has been recorded and what the next response will be.

## Review continuity and dignity

Audit referrals for missing ownership, repeated explanations, privacy gaps, and unclear expectations. Keep the intake humane and concise. Families and referral partners need a dependable next step, not a speculative answer.

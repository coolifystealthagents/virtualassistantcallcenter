## Separate emergency from routine

Ask what happened, where it happened, and whether there is an immediate danger to people or the property. Follow the management company's emergency instructions and escalate urgent conditions rather than attempting a repair diagnosis.

## Capture access and contact details

For routine work, note the unit or property, the resident's preferred contact method, access permission, and useful timing constraints. Avoid recording unrelated personal information in the maintenance note.

## Close the loop clearly

Tell the resident whether the message is being routed, whether a vendor must confirm, or whether the property team will follow up. Repeat the location and callback number so the work order starts with accurate data.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

# Routing bilingual callers without losing the request

*October 22, 2026*

A caller who reaches someone who does not share their language often hangs up or repeats the story three times. Routing notes and a short interpreter process prevent both.

## Ask for the preferred language

Record the language the caller prefers, not the language they used first. A bilingual caller may start in one language and prefer another for details.

Add the preference to the customer record so the next call starts correctly.

## Use a qualified interpreter

For sensitive or technical calls, use the approved interpreter service rather than a colleague who speaks some of the language. Brief the interpreter with the call reason and the confidentiality rules.

Do not ask a family member to interpret medical, legal, or financial details unless the customer insists and the policy allows it.

## Pass context, not just the language

The handoff note should include the caller's name, the language, the reason for the call, what was already verified, and the next action. The receiving agent should not restart the verification.

## Confirm understanding

Use a repeat-back for key details: dates, amounts, addresses, and next steps. Ask the caller to confirm in their preferred language where possible.

## Record the interpreter and the consent

Note the interpreter identifier, the time, and any consent required by policy. Keep the note factual and avoid unnecessary personal detail.

## Review routing gaps

Track calls that were transferred twice, abandoned after a language mismatch, or required an interpreter. The pattern shows whether the phone menu, the staffing, or the language list needs work.

## Define a valid reason to call

Use an outreach record that links every attempt to its source request or approved campaign. It should display permitted purpose, contact window, suppression status, attempt limit, approved message, and responsible business owner.

## Stop and escalate correctly

A stop request suppresses further outreach through the approved system. Wrong parties receive no sensitive context. Disputed consent, hostile responses, vulnerable callers, and legal or regulatory questions go to the compliance or management owner.

## Create the outreach record

Record contact name, number, source, purpose, consent basis where required, timezone, allowed window, suppression check time, attempt number, dial time, identity result, conversation outcome, requested next step, stop request, owner, and follow-up due time.

## Constrain the conversation

The caller may use the approved purpose and answer authorized factual questions. They may not conceal identity, use deceptive urgency, keep selling after refusal, dial outside approved rules, or promise pricing, eligibility, or outcomes beyond their role.

## Check permission before dialing

The workflow should honor applicable calling and consent requirements chosen by the business. Keep the source evidence and current suppression state available to the person placing the call.

## Pilot different contact outcomes

Test a successful follow-up, voicemail with limited disclosure, wrong-party answer, and immediate stop request. Add a record whose timezone is unknown and verify it is held for review.

## Audit respectful completion

Monitor eligible records, suppression checks, attempts, verified contacts, requested outcomes, stop requests processed, wrong-party contacts, out-of-window calls, and overdue follow-ups. Contact rate alone does not measure a respectful workflow.

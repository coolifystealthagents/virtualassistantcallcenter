## Identify the property question

Commercial real-estate callers may ask about a listing, a tour, an existing lease, building access, or a maintenance issue. Capture the property or suite first when known. That context determines whether the message belongs with leasing, property operations, or a named tenant contact.

## Collect business-ready details

Record caller name, company, callback number, property or listing, request, desired timing, and whether the caller represents a tenant, buyer, broker, or vendor. For new interest, note space type and approximate need without promising availability or terms.

## Make tours and callbacks precise

For a tour request, capture preferred windows and attendees, then state whether confirmation is pending. For an active tenant concern, record access details and urgency through the approved process. Every handoff should include the exact requested response and a due time.

## Improve the property queue

Look for calls that lacked a suite, listing, or responsible owner. Fix those fields in the opening questions. Strong intake keeps leasing conversations focused and helps operations respond to tenant needs without unnecessary transfers.

A card-readback script that refuses to expose the full number, offers a safe alternative, and records the request, so the caller is served without a data exposure. The entry should be dated and signed by the agent who handled it. Keep the supporting note with the call record so a reviewer can verify it. The team lead should review the entry at the next shift meeting.

## Key takeaways

- Record the request in the caller's words before explaining the limit.
- Name the owner, the time, and the next step.
- Use the approved verification or secure channel.
- Review the record for patterns, not for individual blame.
- Close the loop with the caller in writing where the policy allows.

## Do not read the full number aloud

Explain that the agent cannot read the full card number aloud, even to the account holder. A full number in a call recording or a shared office is a data exposure. Record the refusal and the reason in plain language. The script should not make the agent sound suspicious of the caller.

Offer the last four digits and the card type instead, and ask the caller to confirm what they see. A partial match is enough for most service questions. Record the confirmation and the time. The script should give the caller a useful check.

## Move the request to a safe channel

Direct a payment change to the approved secure channel, such as the portal or a masked entry form. Record the channel and the reference. A read-back is not a secure verification method. The script should not accept a number spoken aloud for a new payment.

Record the caller's reason for the request, such as a lost card or a suspected fraud. A reason helps the team route the case and spot a pattern. Record the reason and the next step. The script should not dismiss the concern.

## Document the refusal and the alternative

Record the request, the refusal, the alternative offered, and the caller's response. A note that says caller asked for card number is incomplete. Record the time and the agent. The script should show the caller was helped, not only blocked.

Escalate a caller who insists or becomes abusive to the team lead. Record the escalation and the outcome. A repeated insistence may be a fraud signal. The script should protect the agent and the data.

## Review card-readback requests each month

Review the requests for repeats, channels, and outcomes. A pattern may point to a confusing portal or a training gap. Record the count and one change. The review should not expose card data.

Check that no full card number appears in a note, a recording, or a chat. A single exposure is a finding. Record the check and the correction. The next review should show a clean sample.

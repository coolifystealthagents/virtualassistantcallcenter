## Sort service requests quickly

Ask whether the caller needs bookkeeping, payroll, an existing-client update, a document request, or a prospective-service conversation. A short category prevents a general voicemail queue from hiding a time-sensitive client need.

## Protect confidential details

Collect the minimum information needed to identify the caller and request. Do not ask for account credentials, full financial records, or tax conclusions on an intake call. Route sensitive matters to the firm's approved channel.

## Give every call an owner

Close by naming the next internal owner or queue and recording the caller's deadline. If a deadline is unclear, ask one neutral timing question instead of promising a response.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

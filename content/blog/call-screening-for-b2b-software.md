## Identify the reason and account

Ask whether the caller needs a product demonstration, technical support, billing help, renewal discussion, or implementation update. Capture the company and existing account status when the caller provides it.

## Route by urgency and ownership

Support incidents, account changes, and new-business requests need different owners. Record the affected workflow, business impact, and desired next step without diagnosing the issue or promising a fix.

## Leave a useful handoff

A strong message includes the caller's role, company, product area, preferred callback path, and any deadline. Repeat the summary so the caller can correct an important detail before the handoff.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

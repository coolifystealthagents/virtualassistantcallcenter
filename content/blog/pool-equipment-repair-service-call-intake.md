## Locate the property and equipment

Record the service address, owner or authorized contact, callback number, pool or spa location, equipment type, brand and model if visible without risk, and any existing service-plan or work-order number.

## Describe behavior rather than diagnose it

Capture noises, leaks, displayed codes, loss of circulation, heating complaints, lighting issues, and when the caller noticed the change. Ask whether equipment is running, off, or unknown. Do not advise opening panels, handling chemicals, or touching wet electrical equipment.

## Use the company's safety boundary

Reports of smoke, burning odor, electrical arcing, major flooding, or chemical exposure require the approved urgent route. The intake agent records the caller's observations and follows the script; a technician decides the safe response and repair scope.

## Prepare access and follow-up

Note gate instructions, pets, tenant or community approval, equipment-area access, previous technician visits, and preferred contact window. Read back the request status. A logged work order is not proof of warranty coverage, parts availability, technician arrival, or final price.

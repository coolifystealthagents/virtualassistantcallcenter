## Ask about the visit type

Identify whether the caller needs a routine visit, vaccination, medication question, records transfer, or urgent review. Capture the pet's name, species, and the caller's relationship without trying to assess the condition.

## Use the clinic's urgent path

If the caller reports breathing trouble, major injury, poisoning concern, or another emergency signal, follow the clinic's approved urgent-care direction. Do not diagnose, calculate medication changes, or delay escalation while collecting a long history.

## Confirm the handoff

Repeat the requested service, best callback number, and any immediate instruction provided by the clinic. A calm summary helps the care team prioritize the request and helps the caller know what happens next.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

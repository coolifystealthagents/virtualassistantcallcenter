## Start with the shipment reference

Collect the broker's file number, bill-of-lading or airway-bill reference, origin, destination, mode, caller company, and callback details. Ask which document or status prompted the call. Do not repeat sensitive identifiers beyond the broker's approved verification process.

## Name the document precisely

Record whether the caller mentions a commercial invoice, packing list, arrival notice, power of attorney, entry document, or another named item. State whether the caller says it is missing, rejected, corrected, or awaiting review. Avoid replacing the caller's wording with a customs classification.

## Leave decisions with licensed or authorized staff

A virtual assistant should not choose a tariff code, declare value, advise on admissibility, or promise release. Send those questions to the broker, importer, customs professional, or compliance owner identified in the account instructions.

## Close the loop

Read back the shipment reference, document name, stated problem, requested deadline, and callback number. Mark who owns the handoff and when it was sent. If the caller supplies a file, follow the approved secure-document channel instead of accepting it through an improvised address.

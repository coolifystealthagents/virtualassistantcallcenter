## Match the caller to a workspace conversation

A coworking inquiry may involve a day pass, hot desk, dedicated desk, private office, meeting room, virtual office, or team suite. The receptionist can identify the need, check approved availability, explain published features, book a tour, and route contract or exception questions. They should not promise access, approve an identity exception, interpret a lease, or invent a discount.

The first call should establish which location and product the person means. Multi-location brands often have different hours, amenities, pricing, and access rules. Read back the location before scheduling anything.

Separate prospects from current members needing building access or reporting an incident. A locked-out member requires verification and a security workflow, not a sales intake.

## Capture practical fit without over-collecting

Ask about team size, workspace type, desired start, expected duration, meeting-room needs, business-hours pattern, and accessibility requirements. If the caller asks about receiving mail or registering a business address, flag it for the authorized owner because rules and documentation may apply.

The record should include:

- Caller, organization, and verified contact details.
- Location and workspace product.
- Team size and start window.
- Access-hours and meeting-space needs.
- Tour preference and accessibility request.
- Availability and pricing source checked.
- Contract, mail, security, or exception question.
- Owner and response window.

Do not collect identification documents or payment information in general notes. Use the approved onboarding system when the business is ready for those steps.

## Keep availability and pricing current

Name the system that controls desks, offices, meeting rooms, promotions, and closures. A photographed rate card or old website message should not override the current source. Record when it was checked.

Use exact product states. An office being cleaned, held, under renewal, or expected to open is not available now. Explain whether a quoted rate is monthly, daily, per person, before tax, or subject to a term. State deposits, setup fees, and included hours only from approved material.

Custom packages and discounts belong with the membership owner. If an online offer conflicts with the system, record the page and route the discrepancy instead of choosing the more convenient figure.

## Book tours with access and privacy in mind

Confirm location, local date and time, attendee count, host, entry instructions, and any accessible route. Do not include door codes in open calendar descriptions or voicemail. Send sensitive arrival details through the authorized channel and track delivery.

The host should see enough context to prepare: team size, product interest, start window, and important questions. They do not need unrelated personal history.

When the building operates unattended, define who can approve visitor access and what happens when verification fails. A tour booking should never become a shortcut around building security.

## Route mail and address questions carefully

Virtual-office and mail services can involve identity, carrier, contract, and jurisdiction-specific requirements. The receptionist can explain the published service and arrange review. They should not promise that an address can be used for every registration, license, bank, or government purpose.

Capture the intended general use and send it to the authorized owner. Avoid requesting identity documents over ordinary email or text. Explain the secure onboarding step only after the product fit is confirmed.

If a caller expects packages, large freight, regulated goods, or frequent visitors, flag the requirement. Published receiving limits and building rules should control.

## Handle security and current-member calls separately

A current member reporting lost credentials, a locked door, suspicious activity, or an unknown visitor needs the security path. Verify under the approved process and avoid sharing access codes or member lists. For immediate danger, follow the building’s emergency plan.

The [CISA physical security guidance](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/physical-security) offers general U.S. resources, while the coworking operator must maintain site-specific procedures. Receptionists should know the boundary between sending an approved access-recovery link and manually bypassing a control.

Keep incident details out of the prospect CRM when they belong in a protected security record.

## Build a manager-ready handoff

For example: “Three-person design firm seeks a private office at the north location beginning mid-November, needs two evening meetings monthly, and asks whether business mail registration is supported. Inventory checked at 13:20 and shows one office open for inquiry, not held. Tour confirmed Friday 11:00. Membership manager to answer mail-use and after-hours questions.”

This note supports a focused tour without making promises. Close the call by stating the tour status, location, arrival method, and when unresolved questions will be answered.

If the caller wants a tentative hold, use the operator’s exact hold rule, expiration, and payment process. Do not create an informal hold in a note.

## Review the whole inquiry path

Audit wrong-location bookings, outdated prices, unconfirmed spaces described as available, tour delivery failures, access information exposed, and manager questions left unanswered. Measure inquiry completeness, tour attendance, owner acknowledgment, and repeat contacts caused by unclear status.

Conversion alone is not enough. A signed membership based on incorrect access hours or mail claims creates later support and trust problems. Include accuracy and expectation-setting in quality review.

When the same question recurs, update the public source or script. Version location guides and retire old offers.

## Start with one location

Test a day-pass inquiry, private office, remote tour, accessibility request, mail-service question, inventory conflict, current-member lockout, and calendar outage. Have membership and security owners approve their paths.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a receptionist hold an office?

Only under the written hold rule, with the correct expiration and customer-facing status. An inquiry note is not a reservation.

### Can they promise that an address works for company registration?

No. They can explain the published service and route the caller’s intended use to the authorized owner for review.

### What if a current member is locked out?

Move the call to the verified access-recovery workflow. Do not share codes or bypass building controls through the sales queue.

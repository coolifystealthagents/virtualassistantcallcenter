## Ask only when the cue will help

If you are unsure, ask the caller how they would like their name pronounced. Use neutral wording and accept correction without making the caller repeat an explanation. A pronunciation preference is a service note, not evidence about language, nationality, gender, or identity.

## Write a cue the next assistant can use

Use the organization's approved phonetic style or a short plain-language cue supplied or confirmed by the caller. Keep the official spelling unchanged in its designated field. Add the preferred form of address separately when the system supports it.

## Protect and maintain the note

Store the cue only in an approved customer-service field, not in a public profile or free-form label. Read it in context before the next call, and update or remove it when the customer requests a change. Do not copy pronunciation notes into unrelated systems by default.

Quality reviews should check whether the note improves the interaction and whether agents still confirm identity through the approved process. Respectful pronunciation and identity verification solve different problems.

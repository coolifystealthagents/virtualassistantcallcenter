## Start with the account reference

Ask for the caller's name, patient name when different, callback number, statement or trip reference, service date, and the relationship of the caller to the patient. Follow the billing company's identity-check policy before discussing any account detail. A virtual assistant can collect the message; authorization decisions stay with trained billing staff.

## Separate the question from the explanation

Label whether the caller is asking about a balance, payment posting, insurance status, itemized statement, duplicate notice, or another issue. Record the caller's own wording and the notice date. Do not interpret benefits, promise an adjustment, or tell the caller that an insurer will pay.

## Preserve urgent context without inventing urgency

Note deadlines printed on a statement, collection notices mentioned by the caller, returned mail, and prior callback attempts. Use the approved escalation route for threats, legal correspondence, or suspected identity misuse. Clinical questions and emergencies require the company's designated clinical or emergency pathway, not a billing guess.

## Close with a named next step

Read back the reference, question category, best contact method, and any stated deadline. Give only the approved response window. The handoff should name the billing queue or owner so the caller is not left with a vague promise that someone will look into it.

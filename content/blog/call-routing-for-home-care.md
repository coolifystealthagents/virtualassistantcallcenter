## Identify the caller and relationship

Ask whether the caller is a prospective client, family member, current client, caregiver, referral partner, or another contact. Capture the service area and requested next step without collecting a full care history on the first call.

## Use a clear urgent path

If a caller reports an immediate safety concern, follow the agency's established escalation direction. Do not provide medical advice or make staffing promises while trying to complete intake.

## Create a respectful handoff

Record availability needs, preferred callback window, and the person who should respond. Confirm that the agency will review service fit and timing rather than guaranteeing coverage before the care team checks.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

## Find the project stage

Ask whether the caller is researching a new installation, waiting on a site visit, asking about an active project, or requesting service. Capture the property area and the requested next step.

## Keep technical claims with specialists

Do not estimate production, savings, permitting outcomes, or equipment suitability on the phone. Record the caller's questions and route them to the qualified project or service team.

## Make scheduling concrete

For a site visit or callback, capture the property's general location, preferred windows, and access constraints volunteered by the caller. Confirm that the team will verify availability before treating the time as final.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

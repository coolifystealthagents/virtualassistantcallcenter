## Identify the grooming request

Ask whether the caller needs a bath, haircut, nail service, specialty grooming, or an existing appointment change. Capture the pet's species, size, coat or service detail volunteered by the caller, and the preferred timing without promising that the groomer can perform a service before review.

## Record safety and handling notes

Note allergies, behavior concerns, mobility limitations, or other handling information the caller chooses to share. Do not diagnose a skin or health issue. Route care concerns to the groomer or veterinary professional the business designates.

## Make the appointment handoff clear

Repeat the requested service, pet name, callback number, and preferred window. Explain whether the business will confirm a slot, request more information, or route the question to the groomer so the caller knows the next step.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

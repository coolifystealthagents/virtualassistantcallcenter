## Start with the service request

Ask what the caller noticed, where the problem is located, and what outcome they want. A recurring indoor concern, a single sighting, and a request for prevention may need different follow-up. Keep the opening short and let the caller describe the issue in familiar words.

## Capture access and safety details

Record the property address, contact number, affected area, pets, children, and access limits when those details affect the next visit. Do not diagnose the cause over the phone. If the caller reports a possible exposure or immediate hazard, follow the company’s approved escalation path instead of improvising advice.

## Make the handoff useful

The next owner should see the caller’s request, location, timing, access notes, and promised follow-up. A message that says “bug problem” forces a second interview. A short, factual note gives the technician or office team a workable starting point.

## Review missed details

Look for repeat gaps such as missing gate instructions, unclear service areas, or requests without a callback window. Update the intake fields when a missing detail changes routing. Keep the process narrow enough that callers can finish it without repeating themselves.

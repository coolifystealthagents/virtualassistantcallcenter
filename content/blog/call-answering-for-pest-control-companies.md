## Clarify the service request

Callers may report an active pest concern, ask about recurring service, request an inspection, or change an existing appointment. Identify the property type and requested outcome before discussing availability. This gives the field team context without making a remote assessment.

## Capture facts that change routing

Record name, callback number, service address, property type, pest concern in the caller’s words, access notes, and timing. Ask about children, pets, or occupancy only where the company’s approved safety process requires it. Never promise a treatment result or give unapproved application advice.

## Connect the right owner

New inquiries can follow the estimate or inspection path. Existing customers should reach the service record owner. A concern that meets the company’s urgent criteria should use its designated escalation. Every callback needs a named owner and a realistic expectation.

## Check for avoidable repeats

Review repeat callers, incomplete addresses, and appointments that lacked access information. Update the opening script around the most common omission. Better intake helps the field team arrive prepared and keeps the caller from repeating a stressful problem.

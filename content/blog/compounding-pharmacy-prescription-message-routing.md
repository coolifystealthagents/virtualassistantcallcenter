## Verify the message context

Use the pharmacy's approved identity procedure, then capture caller name, patient name, date of birth if permitted, callback number, prescriber, and prescription or order reference. Avoid repeating sensitive details where others can hear them.

## Choose a message lane

Classify the call as refill request, new-prescription status, prescriber clarification, shipping question, billing issue, storage concern, or request for a pharmacist. Record the question verbatim. An assistant must not interpret ingredients, dosing, interactions, beyond-use dates, substitutions, or expected effects.

## Recognize when the script ends

Side effects, dosing mistakes, damaged or temperature-exposed packages, and urgent medication needs require the pharmacy's defined pharmacist or emergency escalation. Do not assess severity. Follow the written path and preserve the caller's words and time of contact.

## State what happens next

Read back the callback number and message category. Say whether the note was routed to a pharmacist, fulfillment, shipping, billing, or prescriber-support queue, using only an approved response window. Logging a request does not mean a refill, compound, or shipment is approved.

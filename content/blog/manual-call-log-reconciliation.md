## Outage work remains customer work

When the phone system or CRM is unavailable, a virtual call team may use an approved manual log. Restoring the tool does not complete those calls. Notes, promised callbacks, urgent routes, and incomplete intake must be moved into the normal queue with their original context and without launching duplicate actions.

Plan reconciliation before an outage. Define the temporary format, storage, permitted fields, identifiers, owner, and destruction or retention process. A loose sheet or private spreadsheet created during pressure can expose customer information and make later recovery unreliable.

## Use a minimal outage record

Capture time and time zone, temporary identifier, caller name as provided, safe callback route, stated purpose, approved priority trigger, action already taken, next owner, and consent for voicemail where needed. Mark fields unconfirmed when the assistant could not read them back.

Do not recreate the entire CRM on paper. Collect only what supports continuity. Sensitive details should use the business's restricted fallback or wait for the secure system. An outage does not expand the receptionist's authority or data permissions.

## Keep sequence and source visible

Number manual records in order and identify the assistant and temporary channel. When transferring them later, retain the original call time and label the source "outage log." The system-entry time should not replace the customer event time.

Avoid polishing notes into certainty. Spelling, category, and contact information may need confirmation. Store the caller's wording separately from the route label. If two assistants recorded the same call, link the possible duplicate rather than merging it without review.

## Assign live ownership during the outage

A manual list still needs owners. Mark whether the receptionist completed the permitted action, contacted an on-call person, promised a callback, or awaits review. Use a visible method for acceptance so items do not disappear when the sheet changes hands.

At shift change, count and read through every open record. The incoming owner should accept the physical or secure temporary log and compare the sequence. Missing identifiers need immediate investigation while the context is recent.

## Freeze automations before import

Restoring records can trigger texts, emails, lead sequences, reminders, or duplicate callbacks. The system owner should define a staging state that allows data entry without customer contact. Do not bulk import until the effect of each workflow is understood.

If staging is unavailable, enter high-priority open work individually under supervision. The goal is not fastest data entry. It is controlled recovery that preserves the service already delivered and prevents a second conflicting promise.

## Match before creating

Search permitted identifiers to see whether the caller reconnected or another integration created a record during the outage. A phone number alone may represent several people. Compare time, stated purpose, location, and internal references without disclosing existing details to an unverified caller.

Link the manual event to an existing task when it is clearly the same request. Create a new record when it is distinct. Mark uncertain matches for an authorized owner. Do not delete the manual source until reconciliation is checked.

## Enter facts and actions separately

The normal record should show what the caller stated, what the assistant did during the outage, what remained unavailable, and what must happen next. A booked request is not a confirmed appointment if the calendar was offline. A message sent to an on-call channel is not an accepted escalation without acknowledgment.

Use statuses that describe those differences. This prevents managers from assuming the outage queue contains finished work merely because it has been entered into the CRM.

## Reconcile callback promises

List every callback commitment by due state, safe channel, and owner. Check whether the caller already returned the call and whether another worker contacted them. Cancel duplicate attempts before reopening automations.

When the original expectation can no longer be met, the authorized owner should approve an update. The virtual assistant can communicate that update but should not invent a new deadline or compensation.

## Verify totals and gaps

Compare phone-system events, temporary identifiers, manual pages or records, restored CRM entries, and open tasks. Account for abandoned and missed calls that never produced a manual note. Exact agreement may be impossible when systems failed, so document exclusions and unknowns.

Two people can perform entry and review for high-risk lanes. The reviewer checks source, timestamp, ownership, and automation state rather than retyping every field. Correct errors with an audit trail.

## Retire the temporary channel safely

After reconciliation and review, restrict or dispose of manual records according to the approved retention process. Do not leave screenshots, downloaded sheets, or printed pages in ordinary workspaces. Revoke temporary access and remove the outage template from active use.

Preserve only the evidence required by policy and improvement review. The call team should know that the normal system is again authoritative and when that switch occurred.

## Learn from the recovery

Review which fields supported a decision, which were missing, which created privacy risk, and where duplicate work occurred. Update the template and ownership map. Avoid claiming a technical root cause unless the system owner established it.

Measure the number of manual records, reconciled tasks, unresolved items, duplicate contacts, and missing events with clear denominators. These local counts guide the business; they are not public benchmarks.

## Define the finish line

Reconciliation is complete when every temporary record is matched or entered, original timing and source remain visible, open work has an accepted owner, duplicate outreach is suppressed, counts and gaps are reviewed, and the temporary channel is retired safely.

That finish line turns an improvised outage log back into dependable call-center work without pretending the outage never happened.

## Lead with calm clarity

Use a simple greeting and ask what the family needs now: immediate assistance, service planning, document guidance, visitation information, or an existing arrangement. Do not force a caller through a long script before offering the appropriate handoff.

## Protect dignity and privacy

Record names, callback details, and the requested next step accurately. Avoid asking for unnecessary personal history or discussing arrangements beyond the information the caller offers and the funeral home's process requires.

## Escalate without delay

If the caller needs immediate staff assistance, identify the on-call or designated team path. Confirm who will follow up and avoid making promises about timing, services, or legal requirements that staff must confirm.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

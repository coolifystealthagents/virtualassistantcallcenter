# Setting a call queue reopen checklist for the morning shift

## Start with the queue and the greeting

Open the queue before the first scheduled shift and confirm that the greeting, the hours and the routing menu are correct. A stale holiday message or a closed queue tells callers that nobody is ready. Test the main number from an outside line and listen to the full greeting. Record the time of the test and the name of the person who completed it.

Check that the overflow rule and the voicemail fallback are active. A queue that opens without a fallback can send a caller into a loop during the first busy hour. Confirm the expected wait message and the callback offer. The morning test is short and it prevents a public failure that is hard to explain later.

## Confirm the roster and the coverage

Compare the scheduled agents with the people who have actually logged in. Note any absence, late arrival or shift change that affects coverage. Call the backup before the queue opens if the coverage is below the plan. A supervisor who waits for the first missed call to check the roster is already behind.

Record the primary owner and the escalation contact for the shift. The owner should know which queues are live and which are paused. If a queue is paused, confirm that the caller hears a clear message and has a route to leave a request. A silent pause is worse than a slow answer.

## Open the callback list

Review the callback requests that carried over from the previous shift. Sort them by the promised window and identify the ones that are already late. Assign an owner to each late request before the new calls arrive. A callback list that is opened after the queue is busy will stay behind all day.

Check that the contact details are complete and that the caller did not withdraw consent. Remove duplicates and mark the requests that have already been answered. A clean list gives the shift a realistic view of the work. The first hour should not be spent reconstructing the previous shift.

## Review the knowledge notes

Read the overnight notes and the changes that affect today's calls. A price change, a service outage or a new policy can make yesterday's answer wrong. Confirm that the knowledge article and the script match the current decision. If they do not, pause the affected answer and route the question to the owner.

Tell the agents which notes are new and which are still under review. Do not expect them to read a long document during the first call. A five-minute briefing with two or three changes is more effective. The goal is a shared answer, not a shared pile of text.

## Test the callback and transfer paths

Place a test callback to a number the team controls and confirm that the caller ID and the voicemail greeting are correct. Test a warm transfer to the primary owner and to the backup. A transfer path that fails in a test will fail for a caller. Record the result and fix the route before the queue opens.

Check the after-hours message and the emergency route even if the shift is daytime. A queue can close early because of an outage or a staffing gap. The team should know how the caller reaches a person when the normal path is unavailable. The fallback should be tested on the same morning.

## Brief the shift in five minutes

Hold a short briefing before the first call. Cover the coverage, the callback list, the knowledge changes and the escalation contact. Ask one question to confirm that the team heard the changes. Keep the briefing focused and end it on time so the agents can open their tools.

Do not use the briefing for a long performance review or a general discussion. Those topics belong in a separate meeting. The morning briefing is a handoff, and its value is in the shared facts. A short, consistent briefing is easier to sustain than a weekly marathon.

## Record the opening result

Log the queue test, the roster check, the callback count and any open issue. Note the time the queue became ready for callers. If something was late, record the cause and the person who resolved it. The record helps the next shift see whether the opening is improving.

Review the opening records after a week and look for the same delay. A repeated late login or a repeated knowledge gap is a process problem. Fix the step rather than asking the team to try harder. The checklist should make the morning calmer, not add another layer of reporting.

## Close the loop with the prior shift

Send a short note to the prior shift about the callback items that were completed and the ones that remain. Thank the team for the handoff without turning the note into a score. The next shift should know what happened after the handoff. A closed loop makes the callback list a shared responsibility.

If the prior shift left a serious gap, raise it with the supervisor rather than in a group message. The goal is a better process, not public blame. A morning checklist works when the team trusts that the record will be used fairly. That trust is built one opening at a time.

## Close the queue with the same care

A queue closing deserves a short checklist as well. Confirm the greeting change, the voicemail owner and the callback list handoff before the last agent leaves. Record the time the queue closed and the person who confirmed it. A clean close makes the next opening easier.

If a queue closes early, tell the team and the callers who are waiting. Update the message and the website hours together. A caller who reaches a closed line without an explanation may not try again. The close is part of the service, not the end of the shift.

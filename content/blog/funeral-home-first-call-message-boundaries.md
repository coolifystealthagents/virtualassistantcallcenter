## Begin with the person calling

Ask for the caller's name, relationship to the person who died, callback number, and current location. Speak plainly and leave pauses. If the caller is not ready to answer a question, record that and move to the minimum details needed for a director to return the call.

## Record facts, not conclusions

Capture the deceased person's name, place of death, time if known, and the organization or official currently present. Do not decide whether a case is ready for transfer or explain permits, medical examiner rules, pricing, or contract terms unless the funeral home has supplied exact approved language.

## Use the on-call route

Follow the location's written schedule and contact order. If the first director does not acknowledge the message within the stated interval, move to the next contact. Keep family calls separate from vendor questions and routine service inquiries so the on-call queue stays clear.

## Make the handoff specific

Read back the name, location, callback number, and expected next contact. Do not promise an arrival time. The final note should show when the call arrived, who received the escalation, and whether the director acknowledged it.

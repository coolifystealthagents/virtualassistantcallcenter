## Start with the requested outcome

People call a physical therapy clinic to ask about a first visit, referral paperwork, existing appointments, or a change in mobility needs. Open by identifying the desired outcome. A request for an evaluation follows a different path from a question about an established plan of care.

## Ask only decision-making questions

Capture the caller’s name, callback number, patient relationship, preferred location, and requested timing. Note referral status without interpreting clinical eligibility. Ask about communication or mobility accommodations when relevant to the visit. Keep clinical details to the approved minimum and route diagnosis or treatment questions to qualified staff.

## Protect the handoff

The message should state what the caller needs, what information is still missing, and who must respond. For an appointment request, record the acceptable days and times rather than promising a slot. For an urgent concern, follow the clinic’s approved escalation language and connect the caller to the designated clinical path.

## Review access and follow-up

Sample calls for accurate identity handling, respectful language, complete appointment notes, and timely ownership. A useful review asks whether the next person could act without calling the patient back just to understand the original request.

A source-bounded call center support guide for call center managers: quality sample without unsupported decisions.

## Why quality sample needs its own control

A call center support team can complete a task and still leave the next person unable to verify it. The quality sample control exists to make the work reconstructable from a source, a decision, and a close.

The cost is not only rework. When the quality sample record is thin, managers spend time asking what changed, customers hear a different version, and the next reviewer has to rebuild the story from memory.

Treat the control as a queue item with an owner, a due time, and a close condition. A task that has no close condition becomes an open loop that survives the week.

Review one quality sample case end to end in the weekly meeting. Check the source, the decision, the destination record, and the confirmation rather than only the completion count.

Write the next action as a sentence with an owner and a time. "Review the quality sample exception" is not a next action; "Mina checks the missing source by 2pm and routes it to the owner" is.

## Start with the source record

Begin with the record that governs quality sample. Record who created it, the version or date observed, the field it supports, and the owner who can confirm it.

Keep the source and the interpretation in separate fields. A forwarded message or a verbal confirmation can be a lead, but it is not proof that the record changed.

When two sources disagree, preserve both values and label the discrepancy. Do not overwrite the earlier value to make the report look clean.

If the quality sample rule changes, keep the earlier classification and apply the new rule to later cases. A definition that changes quietly makes the trend unreadable.

## Separate preparation from approval

Name the owner before the VA prepares any action. The VA may collect, compare, label, calculate, and route; the accountable owner decides whether the evidence is enough.

Write the boundary into the task brief. Name the systems the VA may use, the fields they may change, and the questions they must route.

A complete packet is not an approval. A drafted message, a proposed schedule, or a reconciled total still needs the named owner decision when policy or risk is involved.

Keep a named backup for the quality sample owner. Coverage during leave or a time-zone gap should be written into the routine, not improvised when the owner is unavailable.

## Build the routine

Build a short daily routine for quality sample: open the queue, check the source, record the exception, and leave a next action with a time.

Use the same fields every day. Consistency makes the work comparable and lets a reviewer spot a missing source without reading the whole thread.

Keep the queue small enough to finish. A long queue hides the oldest exception and makes the monthly report look better than the operation.

Do not copy a value from a prior quality sample record because it looks familiar. Reopen the source, confirm the version, and record what changed.

## Handle exceptions before they age

Define reason codes for quality sample: missing evidence, identity mismatch, conflicting record, stale version, unavailable owner, access failure, and scope change.

Preserve the original request and the correction. A later reviewer should see what was known when the first action was taken.

If the exception affects pay, safety, access, legal, or a customer commitment, stop the dependent action and route it. Urgency is not permission to work around the control.

Close the loop with the person who raised the quality sample issue. A record that is internally complete but never confirmed by the requester is only half closed.

## Verify, close, and review

Close quality sample only when the destination record shows the approved change. A sent message or a closed ticket is not proof that the schedule, ledger, access, or assignment changed.

Reconcile open cases at the end of the day and explain the carryover. A case that is silently carried forward becomes an aging exception.

Review a sample each month. Check the source, the approval, the execution, and the confirmation. A repeated gap should change the procedure, not only the coaching.

Record the exact quality sample source and the time it was observed. A later reviewer should be able to tell whether the value was current when the first action was taken, even if the record has changed since.

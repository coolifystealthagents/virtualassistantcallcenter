## Define waitlist eligibility

Write down which appointment types, locations, providers, and date ranges may use a waitlist. Capture the caller's acceptable windows and contact preference, but do not promise that a slot will open. Confirm whether voicemail or text may include appointment details under the business's privacy policy.

Keep the waitlist separate from confirmed appointments. Its status values should make the difference obvious: waiting, offer sent, offer expired, booked, declined, or removed.

## Offer one slot with a clear deadline

When a cancellation creates availability, select callers according to the approved rule. Contact one caller or a defined group, depending on the scheduling model, and state how long the offer remains open. The assistant must not imply that an unheld slot is reserved.

Record the offer time and expiration in the shared scheduling record. If several assistants make calls, this timestamp prevents overlapping promises. Before confirming acceptance, recheck that the slot remains available and follow the ordinary booking controls.

## Close the loop for every outcome

Mark declined and expired offers promptly so the next eligible caller can be contacted. If the caller accepts, send the normal confirmation and remove any duplicate waitlist entries. If the caller asks to leave the list, honor the request across all related entries.

Review waitlist performance using filled slots, expired offers, response time, and booking corrections. Do not reward speed that produces duplicate bookings. The useful outcome is a confirmed appointment with a traceable offer history and an accurate schedule.

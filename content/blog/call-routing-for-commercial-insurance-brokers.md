## Use the caller’s business need

Commercial insurance lines create several similar-sounding calls: certificate requests, policy questions, renewal preparation, claims notifications, and new-business inquiries. Route by need and account context. A certificate request may belong to service staff, while a coverage question requires a licensed professional.

## Capture policy context without overcollecting

Ask for the caller’s name, company, callback number, account or policy reference if available, and the exact requested action. Record deadlines for certificates or renewals. Do not interpret coverage, bind changes, promise approval, or request sensitive information outside the broker’s approved process.

## Make escalation explicit

Claims and urgent loss reports should follow the broker’s stated immediate path. Routine requests can enter a queue with an owner and due time. Read the message back to confirm the business name, deadline, and requested response, especially when several locations share an account.

## Improve from routing exceptions

Track transfers, missing account references, deadline misses, and repeat callers. Use those patterns to clarify the menu and update the routing map. The best workflow makes professional judgment easier to reach without pretending that every question is routine.

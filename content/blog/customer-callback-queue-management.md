## Treat every promise as queued work

Use a callback board with columns for new, assigned, in progress, waiting on caller, completed, and closed. Every open card needs a named owner and a next review time, including cards waiting on someone else.

## Set up the callback board

Record callback ID, source call ID, caller, verified number, safe contact window, reason, requested outcome, priority basis, promise made, promised deadline, owner, attempt count, latest attempt, voicemail permission, waiting reason, next review, and closure code.

## Separate assignment from authority

Queue coordinators may assign, reprioritize under written rules, and close documented duplicates. They may not claim an issue is resolved, extend a promise without contacting the caller, expose queue details, or make the specialist’s decision.

## Order work by commitment and impact

A queue is a promise ledger, not a list of phone numbers. Sorting first by due commitment, then by documented impact and age, helps prevent the newest or loudest request from consuming all attention.

## Recover overdue and unreachable items

An overdue promise triggers supervisor review and a truthful status contact. Wrong numbers, repeated no answers, linked incidents, and new safety information each have distinct statuses so they are not quietly marked complete.

## Report queue reliability

Measure callbacks due, completed on time, overdue count and age, first successful contact, attempts per completed callback, reassignment, reopened items, and closures by reason. Separate contact success from issue resolution.

## Stress test the queue

Load the board with two calls due at once, one wrong number, one caller unavailable until later, and one item whose owner is absent. Observe whether any card becomes unowned.

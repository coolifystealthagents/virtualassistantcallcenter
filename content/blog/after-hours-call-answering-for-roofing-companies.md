## Sort the night call quickly

Roofing after-hours calls commonly involve a new estimate, storm damage, an active project, a missed crew, or access to a property. Ask what happened and what the caller needs next. The workflow should identify concerns that need the company’s urgent path without turning the intake into a remote inspection.

## Record the roof and property context

Capture name, callback number, property address, building type, whether the caller is owner or tenant, visible issue in the caller’s words, and timing. Note access constraints and active project references when approved. Do not promise a crew, coverage outcome, or repair diagnosis.

## Give the next owner a useful message

State the requested response, urgency, and any expectation given to the caller. Put routine estimates into the next-day queue and use the designated escalation for immediate hazards. A named owner is more useful than a generic “someone will call.”

## Review storm-season patterns

Compare repeat calls, incomplete addresses, and unclear expectations. Improve the opening around the pattern that causes the most delay. A calm, factual after-hours record helps the morning team prioritize without making promises the field team cannot keep.

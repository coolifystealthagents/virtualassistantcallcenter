# A phone intake note standard the next team can use

*October 22, 2026*

A call note is often the only record of what a customer asked for. When it says "called about order", the next person has to call the customer again. A standard turns the note into a handoff.

## Use a fixed field set

Record the caller, the verified identity level, the reason for the call, the relevant reference, what was checked, the outcome, and the next action with a date.

Add the call channel and the time. For sensitive calls, note the verification method rather than repeating sensitive data.

## Write in plain, factual sentences

Describe what the caller said and what the assistant did. Separate the caller's claim from the assistant's observation: "caller says the parcel arrived damaged" is different from "parcel is damaged."

Avoid internal shorthand that a new team member will not recognize.

## Capture the next action

A note without a next action is a dead end. Name the person, the action, and the date. If no action is needed, say that explicitly.

Where the call requires another team, record the handoff and the reference so the customer does not repeat the story.

## Keep the note short enough to read

Aim for the minimum that lets another person act. Long narrative notes hide the decision. Use a short summary line at the top, then the supporting facts.

## Audit the notes monthly

Sample calls across queues and score the fields, the clarity, and the next action. Share the pattern with the team, not individual scores, unless coaching requires it.

A consistent note standard reduces repeat calls and makes call quality review possible.

A call-back script that gives the caller a real window, records the owner, and sets a fallback, so a promise does not become a missed call. The entry should be dated and signed by the agent who handled it. Keep the supporting note with the call record so a reviewer can verify it. The team lead should review the entry at the next shift meeting.

## Key takeaways

- Record the request in the caller's words before explaining the limit.
- Name the owner, the time, and the next step.
- Use the approved verification or secure channel.
- Review the record for patterns, not for individual blame.
- Close the loop with the caller in writing where the policy allows.

## Offer a window, not a vague soon

Offer a specific window such as this afternoon or before noon tomorrow, based on the team's actual capacity. A promise of soon is not a commitment the caller can plan around. Record the window and the time zone. The script should use the same windows the team can meet.

Ask whether the window works for the caller and record the answer. A call-back that arrives during a meeting is a second failed contact. Record the preferred number and the safe time. The script should confirm the channel before ending the call.

## Record the owner and the reason

Name the person or the queue that will make the call and the reason for the callback. A general queue with no owner is a promise nobody keeps. Record the owner and the case summary. The note should be enough for the caller to receive a useful call.

Record the caller's question and the answer already given. A callback that repeats the first conversation wastes the caller's time. Record the unresolved item and the next step. The script should show what remains.

## Set the fallback and the escalation

Record what happens if the owner cannot call in the window, such as a second owner or a message with a new time. A missed callback without a fallback is a broken promise. Record the fallback and the time. The script should tell the caller what to expect.

Escalate a missed callback to the team lead on the same day. Record the escalation and the new commitment. A missed promise that is not reviewed will repeat. The script should make the miss visible.

## Review the promises each day

Review the open call-backs at the end of each shift and record the ones without an owner or a window. A promise that is not on the list cannot be kept. Record the count and the fix. The next shift should start from the open list.

Compare the promised windows with the actual call times. A pattern of late callbacks may point to an unrealistic window or a capacity problem. Record the difference and one change. The review should improve the promise, not punish the agent.

## Identify the appliance and problem

Ask for the appliance type, brand if known, broad problem description, location, and whether the caller is an existing customer. Do not diagnose the fault or tell the caller to attempt a risky repair.

## Separate warranty and service paths

Warranty questions, active appointments, and new service calls may have different owners. Label the request accurately and capture the purchase or service reference only when the shop's process requires it.

## Confirm access and timing

Record preferred appointment windows, parking or access notes, and the best callback number. Tell the caller that the shop will confirm availability and any required preparation.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

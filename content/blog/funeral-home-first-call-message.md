## Slow the call down

A first call may come from a grieving relative, a hospital, or a care facility. Speak plainly and allow pauses. Begin with the caller's name, callback number, relationship to the person who died, the person's name, and the current location. Follow the funeral home's approved wording and collect only the facts on its first-call form.

The assistant should not discuss prices, legal requirements, transfer timing, embalming, or service availability unless the funeral home has supplied exact approved information. A warm tone does not require filling silence with guesses.

## Verify the contact path

Read back names that may have several spellings and confirm the callback number digit by digit. Ask for the facility contact and room or department only when it is part of the approved intake. If the caller does not know an answer, leave it unknown. Do not pressure them to search for documents during the call.

Explain that the on-call funeral home representative will return the call. Give a response window only when the firm's written instructions provide one. If the caller rings again, add the new contact time to the same message rather than starting over.

## Escalate and confirm acknowledgment

Send the message to the named on-call representative through the approved channel. If that person does not acknowledge it, follow the backup sequence. Do not forward sensitive details into a broad group chat simply to get a faster reply.

The handoff is complete when an authorized representative acknowledges ownership, not when the assistant presses send. Keep the record concise, restrict access, and flag any broken contact number for correction before the next overnight shift.

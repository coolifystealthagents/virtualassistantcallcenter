## Let the caller set the pace

A family may call before they know what to ask. Start with the caller's name, a safe callback number, the person who died, and whether a funeral director has already been involved. Use the family's wording. A short silence is better than racing through a checklist.

## Separate facts from decisions

Record where the person is now, which organization is involved, and any time-sensitive request. Do not advise on disposition, contracts, benefits, prices, or legal paperwork. Those questions belong with a licensed or authorized funeral home representative.

## Protect private details

Collect only the information the approved script requires. Avoid repeating sensitive facts where others can hear them, and never disclose arrangements to an unverified caller. If identity or authority is unclear, note the request and ask a director to return it.

## Close with an honest status

Read back the callback number and the immediate request. Say whether the note is queued, a transfer was accepted, or a director has confirmed a return call. Do not turn an internal target into a promise to a grieving family.

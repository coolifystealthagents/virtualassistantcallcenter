## Anchor the call to a site and report

Record property name, full address, caller role, callback number, inspection date, report or work-order number, and inspecting company. If the caller reads a deficiency, preserve the exact wording and item identifier rather than paraphrasing technical language.

## Ask about reported system status

Note whether the caller reports a leak, alarm, supervisory signal, tagged valve, out-of-service component, planned shutdown, or no current impairment. Do not instruct anyone to operate a valve, silence equipment, or reset a system. Emergency and impairment calls follow the contractor's written escalation procedure.

## Keep compliance claims out of intake

Capture the authority, insurer, property manager, or tenant deadline as stated and identify the source document. An assistant cannot decide whether a condition violates code, what repair is required, or whether a deadline has been satisfied.

## Send a usable review packet

List report availability, photos, site contacts, access hours, tenant restrictions, and requested service timing. Read back what is attached and what is still missing. The service or inspection manager owns scope, quote, scheduling, and any communication with an authority having jurisdiction.

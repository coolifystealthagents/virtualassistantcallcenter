## Define the project in plain language

Callers may ask for help with a move, a single room, paperwork, or a household reset. Ask what they want changed and which space is involved. Do not assume that a request for organizing includes packing, hauling, cleaning, or disposal.

## Gather the details that affect scope

Capture the service address, preferred contact method, room or project type, timing, and any access constraint. If the caller has a deadline, record the date as stated. Avoid asking for sensitive household details that the next owner does not need.

## Keep expectations accurate

The person taking the call should explain only the approved next step. If an organizer must review photos, a project description, or availability, say that clearly and assign the follow-up. Do not promise a result before the team has assessed the work.

## Write a handoff another person can use

A good note contains the caller’s goal, location, timing, contact details, and open question. Review a sample of inquiries for vague scope or missing ownership. When the same question keeps returning, revise the opening prompt or the information page that callers receive.

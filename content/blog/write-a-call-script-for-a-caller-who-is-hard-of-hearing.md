A caller who is hard of hearing may hear some words clearly and miss others. The problem is rarely solved by speaking louder, because volume does not fix clarity. A good script slows the exchange, confirms the important facts, and gives the caller more than one way to receive information. It also keeps the agent from guessing when a detail was not heard.

## Key takeaways

- Ask how the caller prefers to communicate before changing the pace.
- Speak slightly slower and use short sentences instead of shouting.
- Read back names, numbers, dates, and addresses one item at a time.
- Offer a text message or email confirmation when the caller agrees.
- Treat a relay call as a normal call and speak to the caller, not the operator.

## Open by asking what helps

Begin with a normal greeting and then ask a simple question. "Would it help if I spoke a little slower?" gives the caller control without making an assumption. Some callers will say yes, some will ask for a text message, and some will say the line is fine. Accept the answer and continue with the call.

Do not ask about a diagnosis or comment on how the caller sounds. The useful question is about the call, not the person. If the caller mentions hearing aids or a cochlear implant, acknowledge it briefly and ask whether anything about the call should change. The caller decides how much to explain.

## Adjust pace and sentence length

Slow down by a small amount and pause between ideas. Short sentences are easier to follow than a long sentence with several clauses. Say one fact, stop, and let the caller respond. This rhythm feels natural once the agent practices it for a few calls.

Increase volume only if the caller asks. Shouting distorts the microphone and makes consonants harder to understand. If the line is poor, offer to call back on a better connection or to continue by text. A clear connection is more useful than a louder voice.

## Read back one item at a time

Names, phone numbers, dates, and addresses need a separate readback. Say the item, pause, and ask the caller to confirm or correct it. Do not combine four facts into one sentence and ask for a single yes. The caller may catch the first item and miss the rest.

Use the same approach for appointment instructions. State the day, then the date, then the time, and pause after each part. Ask the caller to repeat the part that matters most. Write the confirmed version in the record and mark that it was read back. The [noisy public place script](/blog/write-a-call-script-for-a-caller-in-a-noisy-public-place) offers related wording for calls with poor audio.

## Offer a written confirmation

Many callers who struggle on the phone can read the details without difficulty. Offer to send a short text or email with the date, time, location, and next step. Ask which channel the caller prefers and confirm the address before sending. Keep the message brief and avoid abbreviations that could be misread.

A written confirmation does not replace the call. It gives the caller a record to check later and reduces the chance of a missed appointment. Tell the caller when the message will arrive and what to do if it does not. Record that consent was given for the message.

## Work with a relay service

A relay call may begin with an operator explaining the service. Speak to the caller rather than the operator, and use first person sentences. Allow extra time for the relay to pass each message. Avoid saying "tell him" or "ask her" when the caller is on the line.

The operator may ask the agent to repeat a sentence. Repeat it in the same words rather than summarizing, because the caller hears the relayed version. If the call drops, use the number the relay provides and try again. Treat the relay call with the same patience as any other call.

## Close with a clear next step

End the call by naming one action and one time. "I will send the text within ten minutes, and the office will call you tomorrow morning" is easier to follow than a list. Ask the caller to repeat the part they will act on. Confirm the best number for a return call.

Ask whether anything else needs to be repeated before the caller hangs up. Give the caller time to answer and do not fill the pause. If the caller asks for a written summary, send it and note the request. A calm close is part of the service, not an extra step.

The script should be short enough to use on a busy day. Its value comes from consistency, not from special language. Train the team on pace, readback, and written confirmation. Then review a few calls each month to see where the caller had to ask again.

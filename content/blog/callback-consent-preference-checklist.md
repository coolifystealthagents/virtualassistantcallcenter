## Confirm the requested follow-up

Before ending the call, explain who is expected to follow up and why. Ask whether the caller agrees to a callback and which number should be used. A number displayed by caller ID may not be the safest or preferred return number.

State the realistic contact window and time zone. If the business cannot guarantee a precise time, give an approved range rather than creating certainty the next owner cannot meet.

## Capture channel and message limits

Ask whether voicemail may be left and what level of detail is acceptable. Where text or email follow-up is available, confirm that channel separately under the business's consent process. Permission for one channel should not silently become permission for every channel.

Record the preference in structured fields when possible: callback number, permitted channel, allowed time, voicemail choice, and expiration when the request is temporary. Avoid placing sensitive instructions in free text.

## Hand off and honor later changes

Assign the callback to a named queue with the reason, owner, and due time. The next caller should verify identity according to policy before discussing protected details. If the customer withdraws permission or changes the preferred channel, update the active task and relevant contact settings promptly.

Review failed callbacks for wrong numbers, unapproved voicemail, out-of-window attempts, and duplicate contact. These signals show whether the closing checklist is being followed. A good callback process respects the customer's preference while leaving a clear operational record.

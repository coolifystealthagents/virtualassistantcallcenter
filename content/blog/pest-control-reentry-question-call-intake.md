## Locate the completed service

Ask for the account name, service address, visit date, technician, treatment area, and callback number. Confirm whether the caller is asking before a scheduled visit or after work was completed. Do not assume that two services at the same property used the same product.

## Read only the approved instruction

If the service record contains a customer-facing reentry instruction, read it exactly and identify the treatment it belongs to. If the record is missing, unclear, or conflicts with paperwork the caller has, stop and escalate to the technician or licensed supervisor.

## Treat exposure concerns separately

Use the company's emergency protocol when a caller reports illness, exposure, a spill, or treatment in an unintended area. A phone assistant should not diagnose symptoms or invent cleanup steps. The approved protocol may direct the caller to emergency services, a poison center, the product label, or qualified staff.

## Document the answer source

Record which service entry or approved instruction was used, what the caller asked, and whether a supervisor took over. Read back the callback details and response window. This makes later review possible if the caller contacts the company again.

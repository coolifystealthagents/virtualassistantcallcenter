## Pin down the affected machine

Capture the customer site, building or room, equipment type, asset or serial number if safely visible, callback contact, and the behavior the caller observed. Keep the caller's words. “Won't start” is evidence; “failed control board” is a diagnosis.

## Ask about safe operating status

Record whether the machine is stopped, leaking, unusually hot, producing smoke, or affecting surrounding equipment. Use the service company's approved safety and emergency wording. A remote assistant should not tell someone to remove guards, reset electrical protection, or keep running damaged equipment.

## Keep dispatch status exact

Distinguish request received, technician review pending, visit proposed, visit confirmed, and technician en route. Only an authorized dispatcher changes those states. Parts availability and repair duration also remain estimates until their owners confirm them.

## Build the handoff around access

Confirm receiving hours, parking or loading restrictions, floor or room, site contact, and any shutdown window the caller reports. Read back the request and next owner so the technician does not begin with another round of basic questions.

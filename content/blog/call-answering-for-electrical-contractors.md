## Distinguish service requests

Electrical contractors receive calls about new installations, troubleshooting, maintenance, inspections, estimates, and existing jobs. Ask the caller’s goal first, then route to the correct schedule or project owner. Safety concerns must follow the company’s approved urgent instructions.

## Capture site and access information

Record the caller’s name, callback number, service address, property type, requested work, and preferred timing. Note access restrictions, tenant or owner status, and relevant work-order details when approved. Do not diagnose remotely, quote unapproved work, or promise an arrival window that is not confirmed.

## Turn messages into owned actions

The handoff should state the electrical issue in the caller’s words, the site context, urgency, and next requested response. If dispatch is unavailable, create a queue item with a named owner and a realistic expectation. A clear fallback is safer than an uncertain promise.

## Test the edges

Use examples involving an estimate, an active job, missing address information, and a safety escalation. Review whether the caller reached the right owner and whether the record contains enough detail for the next action.

# Making voicemail transcription useful instead of misleading

*October 22, 2026*

Transcription saves time until it changes a name, a number, or the meaning of a request. The transcript is an aid; the audio is the record. A review routine keeps the difference clear.

## Triage by confidence, not by convenience

Start with messages the system marks as low confidence, messages with numbers or addresses, and messages where the caller sounds distressed. Read the audio for those before acting.

High-confidence routine messages can be handled from the transcript, with a spot check.

## Verify names and numbers

Never call back a number that appears only in the transcript. Confirm it against the caller ID, the customer record, or the audio.

For names and addresses, repeat them back at the start of the callback. The caller will correct a transcription error immediately.

## Capture the request and the tone

A transcript can miss sarcasm, urgency, or a request that was phrased indirectly. Listen for the reason and the emotion, then write a factual note without overinterpreting.

If the message is ambiguous, call back to clarify rather than guessing at the request.

## Keep the audio with the record

Store the voicemail according to the retention policy and link it to the ticket. Do not delete the audio after reading the transcript.

## Review recurring errors

If the same words or names are consistently mis-transcribed, update the glossary or the caller instructions. The fix may be in the system configuration, not the reviewer.

## Measure the effect

Track how often a transcript-only action had to be corrected after listening. A high rate means the team is trusting the text too early.

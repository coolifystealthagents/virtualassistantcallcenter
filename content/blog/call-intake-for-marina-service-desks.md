## Start with the boat and its location

Before discussing the problem, capture the caller's name, callback number, vessel name, marina location, and berth or launch area. Repeat those details back. A service request attached to “the blue boat near the fuel dock” is hard to act on when the shift changes.

## Keep five call lanes separate

Sort the request into berth access, launch or haul-out, mechanical service, billing, or an urgent safety concern. Each lane needs a named destination and a fallback. The assistant can record facts and route the request, but should not promise a launch time, authorize work, interpret a weather restriction, or decide that a vessel is safe to move.

## Treat weather and fuel reports carefully

If the caller reports fire, fuel in the water, an injury, or immediate danger, use the marina's approved emergency instruction. Do not diagnose the scene by phone. For storms or closures, quote only the current approved notice and timestamp it in the call note.

## Close with an operational readback

Confirm the vessel, location, request, urgency stated by the caller, next owner, and expected response channel. Mark estimates as estimates. A short, accurate handoff is more useful than a long note full of assumptions.

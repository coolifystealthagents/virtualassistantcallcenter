## Identify the family’s goal

Childcare calls may concern a tour, enrollment, attendance, a daily message, billing, or an authorized pickup question. The first routing choice should reflect the caller’s goal and relationship to the child. Do not disclose child information until the center’s identity and authorization rules are met.

## Collect practical details

For a tour or enrollment inquiry, record the caller’s name, callback number, child age range, preferred start timing, and requested location. For an existing family, capture the child’s name only through the approved process and note the exact request. Avoid inventing availability or policy exceptions.

## Protect urgent handoffs

Health, safety, and pickup concerns require the center’s designated escalation path. Routine questions can become a callback with an owner and due time. Repeat back the request without adding assumptions, particularly when several children or classrooms could be involved.

## Review trust signals

Check whether callers received consistent privacy language, whether tours were recorded accurately, and whether staff could act from the message. Families should feel heard while the center maintains firm boundaries around child information.

## Check who may receive information

A relative may know the client's address and still lack authorization to receive a care update. Follow the agency's identity and authorization check before confirming a schedule, caregiver name, visit, or client condition. If authorization is unclear, take a callback message without confirming the person is a client.

Use the minimum information needed: caller name, callback number, relationship as stated, reason for contact, and urgency in the caller's own words. Do not collect a detailed medical history in a general call queue.

## Keep observations separate from decisions

If a caller reports a change in the client's condition or a missed visit, record exactly what they observed and when. The assistant should not interpret symptoms, adjust a care plan, or say whether a caregiver did something wrong. Use the agency's urgent escalation route when the written criteria apply.

For schedule-change requests, describe them as requests until an authorized coordinator confirms the change. Never promise a particular worker, visit time, or service that the schedule does not show.

## Confirm ownership of the callback

Send the message to the listed care coordinator, nurse, or on-call owner according to agency policy. Mark who accepted it and when. If nobody acknowledges it in the required window, follow the named backup path.

Repeated family calls should remain attached to the same request. Review unresolved items at shift handoff, with private details visible only to staff who need them. A clean trail lets the agency respond without turning the assistant into a clinical decision maker.

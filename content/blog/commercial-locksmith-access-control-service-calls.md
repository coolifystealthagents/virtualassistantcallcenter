## Identify the site and the problem

Capture the business name, service address, caller name, callback number, and the affected door or system. Let the caller describe the fault before assigning a label such as lockout, failed credential, damaged hardware, or rekey request.

## Verify authority through the approved path

A confident caller is not proof of access rights. Follow the locksmith's account verification and authorization rules. Do not reveal access codes, tenant lists, alarm details, or names from the customer record to help someone pass verification.

## Keep dispatch language precise

Distinguish a request received from a job accepted by a technician. Record access constraints, business hours, safety concerns, and whether someone authorized will meet the technician. The dispatcher owns technician selection and arrival estimates.

## Escalate suspicious requests

Pause when a caller asks to bypass verification, enter an occupied space, copy a restricted key, or conceal the work from an account owner. Preserve the caller's words and route the record to the authorized manager.

## Separate scheduling from care questions

Ask whether the caller wants a visit, needs help with an existing appointment, has a records question, or wants clinical guidance. Each path has a different owner. Do not turn a scheduling call into a clinical interview.

## Capture only useful context

Record the requested service, preferred timing, callback number, and patient status when the office’s approved process requires it. Send care questions to the authorized team. Avoid recording a medical history that does not affect routing.

## Use the concern escalation path

If a caller reports severe or unexpected symptoms, follow the office’s approved urgent language and escalation rule. Document the concern as reported and identify who received it. Do not diagnose or reassure beyond the approved boundary.

## Review the handoff

Check that appointment requests include timing and that care questions reach the right owner. Repeated omissions point to a field or opening question that needs adjustment. Keep the process short and easy to follow.

## Make the first call informative without promising a place

A childcare inquiry call should help a family understand the next step while protecting decisions that belong with the center director. A receptionist can capture the child’s age range, desired start window, schedule need, preferred location, tour availability, and contact details. They may describe approved program information and current inquiry status. Enrollment approval, developmental or medical accommodations, subsidy determinations, and final placement remain with trained owners.

Use precise language. “The toddler inquiry list is open” does not mean “a place is reserved.” “The calendar shows a tour time” does not guarantee enrollment. Families should hear what is confirmed, what is pending, and when the director will respond.

Separate new-family inquiries from current-family messages, absence reporting, billing, and urgent child-safety communication. Those queues have different identity and escalation requirements.

## Collect only what the next decision needs

Ask for the program or location, child’s age or birth month when authorized, desired start period, days or hours needed, and safe contact method. Do not collect medical histories, government identifiers, custody documents, or payment information in a general inquiry record.

A useful inquiry card includes:

- Parent or guardian name and read-back contact details.
- Program, location, and schedule requested.
- Child age band and desired start window.
- Tour preference and communication needs.
- Availability or waitlist source checked.
- Question requiring director review.
- Owner and expected response window.

Record an accessibility request in the family’s words without asking for unnecessary diagnosis. Route it to the center’s designated owner for an individualized response.

## Explain capacity and waitlists accurately

The center should name the system that controls room capacity, enrollment offers, pending acceptances, and waitlist order. A spreadsheet copy or staff memory should not override the approved record. Note the time of the check.

Define what “waitlist” means. Does it preserve order by inquiry date, reflect age and schedule fit, or simply register interest? Explain only the approved process. Never invent a likely wait time from another family’s experience.

If the receptionist may add an inquiry, confirm the requested location and schedule, obtain the required permission, and provide a reference. If a fee or deposit is involved, direct the family to the approved secure process and make clear whether it is refundable or guarantees anything, using only current center language.

## Keep licensing and care decisions with the center

Childcare programs operate under local licensing and safety requirements. In the United States, [ChildCare.gov’s licensing overview](https://childcare.gov/consumer-education/child-care-licensing-and-regulations) helps families find state and territory resources. The center must build its script around the rules that apply to its program.

The receptionist should not decide staff-to-child ratio exceptions, medication arrangements, support for a particular need, custody access, immunization questions, or whether a child is eligible. Capture the question and arrange director review.

If a caller reports an immediate child-safety concern, follow the center’s emergency and safeguarding plan. Do not leave it in an ordinary tour queue.

## Book tours that families can actually attend

Confirm location, local date and time, attendees, arrival instructions, identification or check-in rules, accessibility needs, and rescheduling method. State whether children may attend based on center policy. Send confirmation and track delivery.

The tour record should include the family’s program and schedule interest so staff can prepare. Avoid including sensitive family details that the tour host does not need.

When a tour slot is tentative, label it clearly. If the center closes or the host becomes unavailable, assign the rescheduling task and contact the family through its preferred approved channel.

## Handle common exceptions consistently

Typical exceptions include siblings in different programs, a start date beyond the published horizon, variable work schedules, language needs, custody questions, a request for accommodation, subsidy questions, and a family disputing its waitlist status. Each should have an owner.

A handoff might read: “Parent seeks three days per week for a preschool-age child beginning January and requests communication in Spanish. Inquiry register checked at 09:40; no placement offered. Tour booked for 2 October. Director to confirm variable-day options and language support; parent prefers email.”

The note preserves the family’s goals without promising the outcome.

## Protect family privacy

Use role-based access for inquiry and enrollment systems. Avoid placing sensitive information in shared calendars or ordinary notes. Confirm caller authority before discussing a current child’s record. A person who knows the child’s name is not necessarily authorized.

For voicemail, use minimal approved wording. Do not reveal enrollment, medical, custody, or billing information to an unidentified listener. If contact details change, verify the update before sending documents.

Set retention rules for inquiries that do not proceed. Keeping every family detail indefinitely creates risk without improving service.

## Measure the handoff, not just tour volume

Review correct location, accurate waitlist wording, contact readback, tour confirmation delivery, director acknowledgment, and privacy boundaries. Track inquiries reopened because the schedule or age band was missing, tours booked at the wrong site, and families who had to repeat an accessibility request.

Do not reward tours alone. That can encourage bookings when the basic program fit is absent. Balance conversion with source accuracy, clear expectations, respect, and reliable follow-up.

When repeated questions expose unclear public information, update the source. Give program hours, closure dates, and inquiry rules an owner and review date.

## Pilot with representative family needs

Test an open program, a waitlist, siblings, a changing schedule, an accommodation request, a subsidy question, failed tour confirmation, and an urgent current-family call misrouted to inquiry. Have the director approve every branch.

Review early calls daily and expand only when the receptionist consistently distinguishes inquiry, waitlist, application, and enrollment. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can a receptionist promise a childcare place?

Only if the center has explicitly authorized that action and the enrollment system confirms it. An open inquiry list or tour does not equal placement.

### How should accommodation questions be handled?

Capture the family’s requested support without unnecessary diagnosis and route it to the trained center owner for an individualized response.

### What can be left on voicemail?

Use the center’s minimal approved message. Avoid child, enrollment, custody, medical, and billing details until the recipient is verified.

## Define what deserves a response

After-hours landscaping calls can concern a new estimate, a scheduled visit, a gate or access issue, storm damage, or a change to recurring service. Write the intended outcome for each path. A message is complete when the field team knows the property, request, timing, and next owner.

## Capture property details

Ask for the caller’s name, callback number, service address, current customer status, and requested action. For storm-related concerns, record what the caller observed without promising a crew, safety assessment, or arrival time. Note gate codes or access preferences only in the approved system.

## Route by urgency and ownership

Routine estimates can join the next-business-day queue. A scheduled-service change should reach the route coordinator. A genuine property hazard should follow the company’s approved urgent path. Give a realistic expectation and record it so the next person can honor it.

## Learn from the night queue

Review after-hours calls by reason, missing field, and callback age. If callers repeatedly ask about the same seasonal service, improve the opening explanation and intake choices. The goal is a calm first response and a message the daytime team can use immediately.

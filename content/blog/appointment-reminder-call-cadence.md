# An appointment reminder cadence that reduces no-shows

*October 22, 2026*

Reminders work when they arrive before the decision point. A call the night before helps some appointments and irritates others. The cadence should match the cost of a missed visit and the time the customer needs to rearrange.

## Segment by appointment type

High-value appointments, such as a consultation or a home visit, justify an earlier reminder and a confirmation call. A short service appointment may need only a text on the day.

Record the segment in the booking system so the reminder rule is automatic rather than dependent on memory.

## Choose the timing from the failure mode

If most no-shows happen because the customer forgot, a reminder 24 hours ahead works. If they happen because the time no longer fits, a reminder two or three days ahead gives room to rebook.

Review the pattern before adding reminders. More messages are not automatically better.

## Require a clear confirmation

A reminder should ask for a simple confirmation or a request to reschedule. "See you then" is not a confirmation if the customer did not reply.

Record confirmed, reschedule requested, no response, and cancelled. The status tells the front desk what to do next.

## Handle no response

Set a rule for unresolved reminders: a second attempt, a call, or release the slot. Do not hold a prime slot for a customer who has not confirmed when someone else could use it.

## Protect the customer's preferences

Record the channel the customer chose and any request not to be called. Apply the preference across appointment types.

## Review outcomes monthly

Compare no-show rates by reminder cadence, appointment type, and channel. Keep the change small and measure it; a cadence that works for one clinic may fail for another.

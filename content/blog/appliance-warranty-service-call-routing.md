## Identify the appliance precisely

Capture appliance type, brand, model and serial number when safely available, purchase or installation date, service address, and callback number. Do not ask a caller to move a heavy appliance or reach near electricity, gas, water, or moving parts to find a label.

## Record symptoms in the caller's words

Note what the appliance is doing, when it began, any displayed code, and whether a prior visit occurred. Use the company's safety script for smoke, gas odor, sparking, flooding, or other defined hazards. The assistant should not diagnose the fault or coach a repair.

## Keep eligibility distinct from scheduling

Collect the warranty or contract reference and proof-of-purchase status, but mark coverage as pending until an authorized reviewer confirms it. A requested appointment is not a booked visit. Technician availability, parts, fees, and reimbursement must not be promised from an intake screen.

## Hand off a complete service request

Read back the address, appliance, symptom, access constraints, and preferred contact window. Route the record to the correct manufacturer, dealer, administrator, or service queue and identify any missing document that the caller can provide through an approved channel.

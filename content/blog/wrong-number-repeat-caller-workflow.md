## Verify before classifying the call

A wrong-number call can be a simple dialing error, an outdated directory listing, or evidence that a public number points to the wrong business. Ask which company or person the caller intended to reach and confirm the number they dialed when that information is visible or voluntarily provided. Do not ask for unrelated personal details.

Treat uncertainty carefully. A caller who uses an unfamiliar department name may still be a real customer. The assistant should check the approved business directory before closing the interaction as a wrong number.

## Record only what supports correction

Use a compact disposition with the intended business or department, the apparent source of the number when known, and whether the caller has contacted the team before. Avoid copying sensitive information from a call that does not belong to the business. The operational purpose is to find a routing defect, not to build a profile of the caller.

Repeated reports tied to the same directory, advertisement, former employee, or vendor should create one correction task. Linking calls to that task prevents several assistants from opening duplicate investigations.

## Close politely and fix the source

Once the mismatch is clear, explain that the caller reached the named business and that the team cannot transfer them to an unaffiliated organization. Do not speculate about another company's number. If the issue involves an internal extension or an approved partner, follow the documented transfer rule.

Review repeat patterns weekly. Check directory profiles, campaign numbers, forwarding rules, and published contact pages. Confirm the correction after it is made, then monitor whether the call pattern declines. This turns an irritating queue category into useful evidence about the business's public contact information.

A name-mismatch script that verifies authority without accusing the caller, records the relationship, and explains what can and cannot be changed. The entry should be dated and signed by the agent who handled it. Keep the supporting note with the call record so a reviewer can verify it. The team lead should review the entry at the next shift meeting.

## Key takeaways

- Record the request in the caller's words before explaining the limit.
- Name the owner, the time, and the next step.
- Use the approved verification or secure channel.
- Review the record for patterns, not for individual blame.
- Close the loop with the caller in writing where the policy allows.

## Ask about the relationship, not the discrepancy

Ask how the caller is connected to the account before pointing out the name difference. A caller may be a spouse, a caregiver, or a colleague with valid authority. Record the relationship and the caller's name. The script should not begin with an accusation.

Explain what the business can discuss with an authorized person and what requires the account holder. A vague refusal sounds like a policy excuse. Record the boundary and the option offered. The script should be specific enough to use.

## Verify the authority before sharing details

Use the approved verification method for a third-party caller, such as a documented authorization or a callback to the account holder. Record the method and the result. A name match alone is not authority. The script should not accept a verbal claim.

Record what the caller is allowed to do, such as schedule a service visit but not change payment details. A blanket yes or no is hard to apply. Record the permitted action and the approver. The script should keep the boundary visible.

## Record the mismatch and the outcome

Record the name given, the account name, the relationship, and the resolution. A note that says verified is not enough for the next agent. Record the source and the time. The script should let a reviewer reconstruct the decision.

Tell the caller what will happen next and who will follow up. A caller who is left without a path will call again and escalate. Record the next step and the owner. The script should close with a clear action.

## Review name mismatches for fraud and access risk

Review the mismatches for repeated numbers, unusual requests, and failed verification. A pattern may point to a fraud attempt or a training gap. Record the count and the category. The review should not label every mismatch as fraud.

Check that the verification record is complete for every third-party call. A missing record is the finding even when the call was benign. Record the gap and the fix. The next review should show whether the script is being used.

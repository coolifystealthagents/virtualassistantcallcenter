## Establish location and immediate conditions

Capture the full address, caller relationship to the property, callback number, occupied areas, when the water was noticed, and the source as the caller describes it. Ask whether the caller reports an immediate threat involving people, electricity, structural damage, or contaminated water, then follow the approved emergency wording.

## Do not troubleshoot beyond the script

A call assistant should not tell someone to enter a hazardous space, touch electrical equipment, assess contamination, or guarantee that an area is safe. Route safety and technical questions to the authorized restoration or emergency owner.

## Keep insurance language modest

Record the insurer and claim reference only when the company requires them. Do not promise coverage, reimbursement, scope approval, or a final price. Those decisions belong to the insurer and authorized estimator.

## State the response status exactly

Finish with the address, reported condition, access contact, request timestamp, and whether review, dispatch acceptance, or an arrival window is confirmed. Clear status is especially important when weather creates a long queue.

# After-hours answering rules that keep promises honest

*October 22, 2026*

After-hours callers are usually anxious. They call because something cannot wait until morning, or because they do not know your hours. The script has to separate the two quickly without promising a response the team cannot deliver.

The core rule is simple: state the hours, capture the issue, and say exactly what happens next.

## Open with the real coverage

Say the company name, the fact that the office is closed, and when it reopens. If a live person is available for emergencies, say which categories count as emergencies.

Do not imply that someone is reviewing every message at midnight unless that is true. Callers remember promises and repeat them back in complaints.

## Capture the minimum useful detail

Ask for the caller's name, a callback number, the reason for the call, and whether the issue is urgent. For existing customers, ask for the account reference rather than a full account history.

Read the callback number back digit by digit. A single wrong digit turns a follow-up into a second missed call.

## Route urgent categories

Define the narrow list of issues that escalate after hours: an outage affecting safety, a security incident, a delivery that cannot proceed, or a committed emergency service.

Give the assistant a named escalation path with a phone number and a fallback. Do not let the script end with "someone will call you" when the caller needs a definite time.

## Close with the next step

State the time the callback will happen, or the time the office reopens and will respond. If the assistant cannot commit, say when the caller will hear an update rather than when the issue will be solved.

## Review the overnight log

Each morning, review the calls, the classifications, and the promised times. Note any call that was misclassified as routine or escalated without cause.

The log is the evidence for adjusting coverage. If most "urgent" calls are routine questions, the website or the phone menu is not answering them before the caller dials.

## Classify the caller before the issue

Commercial properties receive calls from tenants, vendors, visitors, and owners. Capture the building, suite, caller role, callback number, and affected system before choosing a queue. This prevents a tenant access problem from being treated like a general leasing inquiry.

## Triage risk, not just urgency

Water intrusion, smoke, a locked emergency exit, and a failed access-control reader need different instructions. A Philippines-based call specialist can follow the manager’s approved decision tree, record observable facts, and contact the designated on-call owner. They should never invent a safety workaround or authorize building access.

## Make vendor calls accountable

For contractor requests, record the property, service area, work-order reference, and promised arrival window. Read the callback route back to the caller. A named owner and timestamp make it easier to identify whether the next step belongs to management, security, or the vendor.

## Set the service boundary

Before selecting a call partner, define which buildings are included, what information may be disclosed, and when a manager must take over. Good coverage is a controlled extension of the property team, not a substitute for emergency response or lease authority.

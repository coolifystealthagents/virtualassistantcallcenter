## Separate first notice from claim advice

The first call should establish whether the caller is reporting a loss, checking an existing claim, or asking about a policy. A Philippines-based call specialist can capture the policy or reference number, loss date, callback details, and preferred channel without interpreting coverage. Coverage decisions belong to the licensed claims team.

## Use an exception-first routing path

Route active losses, missed adjuster callbacks, repair-network questions, and document-submission problems to different owners. A short disposition such as “new loss,” “status request,” or “document issue” gives the receiving team useful context. When a caller reports immediate danger, direct them to the appropriate emergency service rather than treating the call as routine intake.

## Protect the handoff record

Read back only the details needed to confirm the next action. Do not place full medical narratives, payment credentials, or unnecessary identity data in a general call note. The receiving owner should know what was requested, when a response is expected, and how to reach the caller.

## Questions to settle before outsourcing

Ask who can make coverage decisions, which claim systems may be accessed, and what escalation hours apply. The right answering service supports availability while leaving regulated judgments and final communications with the insurer or administrator.

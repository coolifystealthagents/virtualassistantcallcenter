# Verifying caller identity without turning the call into an interrogation

*October 22, 2026*

Phone support has a hard identity problem. The caller's number can be spoofed, the voice is not a credential, and the information requested may be sensitive. The check has to be strong enough to protect the account and short enough to keep the call usable.

The answer is a tiered check tied to the action, not to the caller's confidence.

## Match the check to the request

A question about office hours needs no identity check. A password reset, a payment change, or a medical detail needs a defined verification step.

Write the tiers down. Then the assistant does not have to judge whether a caller sounds legitimate.

## Ask for facts, not secrets

Use information the account holder should know and an attacker is less likely to have: the account reference, the email on file, the last order date, or a recent service interaction.

Never ask for a full password, a one-time code, or a full card number. If the caller offers them, tell them not to share.

## Handle a failed check

If the verification fails, do not disclose whether the account exists. Offer an alternative path, such as an email to the address on file or a callback to the number already registered.

Record the attempt and the reason. Repeated failed attempts on one account are a security signal, not just a support inconvenience.

## Escalate high-risk changes

For payment changes, address changes, or access changes, require a second approval or a written confirmation to the registered contact. The assistant prepares the request; the authorized owner approves it.

## Keep the script short

A verification script should take under a minute in the normal case. If it takes longer, the fields are too obscure or the system is not surfacing the right information.

Review failures monthly. A high failure rate may mean the questions are wrong, not that callers are suspicious.

## Establish the visit type

Appointment setting works best when the caller’s requested visit is clear before availability is discussed. Separate a first visit, follow-up, consultation, paperwork question, and reschedule. If the office uses different providers or locations, capture that context before offering times.

## Gather scheduling preferences

Record the patient’s name, callback number, preferred location or provider, acceptable days, and time window. Ask only the health or insurance questions the office has approved for scheduling. Never promise clinical suitability, coverage, or an appointment until the office’s confirmation rule is satisfied.

## Confirm the next step

Repeat the selected or requested time, location, arrival instruction, and any outstanding item. If the request needs staff confirmation, say that plainly and create a callback record with an owner. A good confirmation gives the caller something concrete to rely on.

## Check for preventable friction

Review calls where people rescheduled, arrived without required information, or received conflicting directions. Fix the clearest recurring issue first. Keep the conversation brief, welcoming, and accurate so the office can focus on the visit itself.

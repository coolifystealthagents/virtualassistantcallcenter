## Clarify the language need

Translation callers may need a written translation, live interpreting, document review, or status on an active project. Ask which language pair and service type are involved. That distinction determines the right specialist and prevents a general inquiry from entering the wrong queue.

## Capture scope without promising delivery

Record caller name, organization, callback number, language pair, document or meeting context, approximate volume, and deadline. Note certification or formatting requirements only when the company has an approved intake field. Do not promise availability, accuracy, turnaround, or legal acceptance before review.

## Preserve project ownership

For an active job, include the reference, current status question, and requested response. For a new inquiry, assign the message to the intake owner with the stated deadline. If a specialist is unavailable, give a truthful next step rather than guessing.

## Review language access

Check whether callers could explain their need comfortably, whether language preferences were recorded, and whether handoffs kept the project context intact. A respectful opening and precise note make specialist follow-up much easier.

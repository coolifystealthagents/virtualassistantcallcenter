## Classify the project inquiry

Ask whether the caller is exploring a new project, checking an active engagement, requesting a document, or calling about a site meeting. Capture project type, location, stage, and the decision-maker's preferred contact path.

## Avoid premature fit claims

A first call can organize facts, but it cannot promise a design timeline, permitting result, or project fit. Record the caller's goals and budget range only if the firm requests it, then route commercial questions to the right principal.

## Prepare the next conversation

Summarize the property or project context, requested service, important date, and next owner. If documents are needed, name them clearly and confirm where the caller should send them.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

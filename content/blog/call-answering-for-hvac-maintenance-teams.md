## Start with equipment and property context

Capture the property type, service address, affected area, equipment type if known, caller role, and callback number. Ask what changed rather than asking the caller to diagnose the system. This gives the technician useful facts without turning intake into a repair instruction.

## Separate discomfort from danger

The company should define its route for gas odor, smoke, electrical danger, extreme temperature, and ordinary comfort complaints. A Philippines-based call specialist can use that approved protocol and direct immediate hazards to appropriate emergency help. They should not troubleshoot unsafe equipment.

## Schedule around access

Record who can provide entry, pets or occupants, preferred windows, and whether a tenant or owner must approve work. Never promise a repair, part, arrival time, or outcome that the dispatcher has not confirmed.

## Make the dispatch handoff clear

Read back the address, issue category, access details, and requested next step. The technician or dispatcher then receives a concise record and the caller knows who owns the response.

## Find the earlier repair order

Ask for the customer's name, callback number, vehicle year and model, repair date, and invoice or repair-order number. If the record is not easy to find, leave that fact in the note instead of attaching the call to a similar name. Repeat the callback number before moving on.

## Capture what changed

Write down the symptom in the customer's words and when it returned. Note warning lights, sounds, leaks, drivability changes, and whether the vehicle is parked. Do not diagnose the fault, blame a part, or promise that the next visit will be free.

## Route safety concerns promptly

Use the shop's urgent path when the caller reports smoke, a strong fuel smell, loss of braking or steering, overheating, or another condition listed in the escalation guide. A phone assistant should not tell the customer that a vehicle is safe to drive. The service adviser owns that decision.

## Close without making a warranty decision

Read back the vehicle, prior work reference, current symptom, location, and best callback time. Tell the customer which adviser or queue will review the call and give only the shop's approved response window. Warranty coverage and repair authorization remain with the shop.

A caller who asks for a price on a service you do not offer is often halfway to a sale with someone else. The agent cannot quote a service the business does not deliver, and should not invent an estimate to keep the caller talking. The call still deserves a clear answer and a useful next step. A short script prevents both a false promise and a dead end.

## Key takeaways

- Confirm the request before saying the service is unavailable.
- State the limit plainly and without apology loops.
- Offer the closest service you actually provide.
- Use an approved referral only when one exists.
- Record the missed request so the owner can review demand.

## Confirm the request first

Repeat the service back before responding. The caller may use a different name for something you do offer. "You are asking for duct cleaning, is that right?" gives them a chance to clarify. A quick check prevents turning away a caller who wanted a standard service.

Ask what outcome the caller needs, not only the service name. A caller asking for window washing may really need clearer light or a cleaner storefront. The need often points to a service you already provide. The label is less important than the result.

Check the service list before you answer with certainty. A new offering may not yet be in the agent's memory. A short lookup is better than a wrong refusal. The [price quote script](/blog/writing-a-price-quote-call-script-without-approving-discounts) assumes the service exists, so confirm that first.

## State the limit plainly

Say that the business does not offer the service, without dressing it up. "We do not provide that service" is clear and respectful. Avoid a long explanation of why, because the caller did not ask for a business review. A short no respects the caller's time.

Do not apologize more than once. Repeated apologies make the caller feel they have caused a problem. A single "I am sorry we cannot help with that" is enough. Then move to what you can do.

Never invent a price range for a service you do not deliver. A rough number can become a commitment in the caller's mind. It can also send them to a competitor with a false benchmark. The honest answer protects both the business and the caller.

## Offer the closest real option

Name the service you provide that comes closest to the need. Explain the difference in one sentence so the caller can judge the fit. "We do not clean ducts, but we do clean the vents that feed them" is a useful contrast. The caller can then decide whether the alternative helps.

Ask whether the alternative solves the problem before pushing it. A caller with a specific requirement may not want a substitute. Respect that answer and move to the referral step. A second attempt is fine, but a third becomes pressure.

Give a clear next step for the alternative. Offer to book, quote, or send details depending on the service. Do not leave the caller to work out how to proceed. A defined next step turns a no into a possible yes.

## Use only approved referrals

Refer the caller elsewhere only when the business has approved that referral. The approval may come from the owner, a partner list, or a written policy. An informal recommendation can create liability and disappointment. Check the list before offering a name.

Say exactly what the referral covers and what it does not. The agent is not vouching for the other provider's price or quality. Frame it as a direction, not a guarantee. "They handle that service, and you would need to confirm the details with them" is honest.

Do not collect a fee or promise a discount through a referral unless the policy allows it. These arrangements need to be documented and disclosed. A casual promise can turn into a dispute. ## Record the missed request

Log the service the caller asked for and the reason you could not help. This record is the only evidence of demand the business will have. Without it, the same request is turned away silently again and again. The note should be short and specific.

Group the missed requests by service and by caller type. A pattern may justify adding a service, a partner, or a script line. One request is noise, while ten in a month is a signal. Share the count with the owner who can act on it.

Review the note with the sales or service lead each month. Ask whether the alternative you offered was a good fit. Their view is more useful than a raw count. ## Keep the tone helpful

A caller who hears a clear no often accepts it quickly. The tone matters more than the wording. Keep the voice even and the pace normal. Do not sound as if the request was unreasonable.

Offer to pass a message to the owner when the service is close to what you do. A short message with the caller's need and number may lead to a new offering. It also shows the caller that the business listens. The owner can decide whether to follow up.

End the call with a clear summary of what happens next. The caller should know whether to expect a call, an email, or nothing more. A clean close prevents a repeat call to check. It also leaves the business with a useful record.

## Identify the child and responsible adult

Capture the child’s age, responsible adult, practice location, broad reason for calling, and callback details. Ask only what the practice needs for routing. A Philippines-based call specialist should not diagnose tooth pain or decide whether a child needs treatment.

## Route urgent concerns by protocol

The practice should define approved wording for trauma, swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, and other urgent reports. Phone coverage can recognize the relevant category and contact the designated dental owner, while immediate danger follows emergency guidance. The caller should never receive improvised clinical advice.

## Make scheduling family-friendly

Offer only confirmed options, record school or caregiver constraints, and explain what paperwork the practice requires. If a child’s records or consent must be verified, make that a named step rather than collecting more sensitive information than necessary.

## Finish with a calm readback

Repeat the child’s first name, requested service, location, preferred times, callback route, and next owner. Families should know whether the dental team will call back or whether a confirmed appointment was actually made.

## Explain the caller's first step

Therapy callers often need reassurance before they share details. Start by explaining what the practice can help with, whether new appointments are available, and what information is needed to request a visit. Avoid promising a clinical fit before a licensed professional reviews the request.

## Protect privacy at the first call

Collect only contact details, preferred appointment times, payer questions, and the caller's stated reason for seeking care. Do not ask callers to describe urgent symptoms in detail or offer clinical guidance. Route immediate safety concerns to the practice's established emergency direction.

## Make scheduling easy to finish

Repeat the requested time window, explain what happens after intake, and confirm the preferred callback method. A short, respectful summary prevents a caller from repeating sensitive information to several people.

## A practical close

Before ending the call, read back the key details, the requested next step, and the callback method. Keep the record factual and route exceptions to the business owner who can make the decision.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should the first call accomplish?

It should identify the caller's purpose, capture only the details needed for routing, and leave one clear next step with an accountable owner.

### Should the intake call give expert advice?

No. Intake should organize information and explain the next step. Advice, eligibility, diagnosis, terms, or exceptions belong with the qualified business team.

## Headline finding
A handoff is accepted when the receiving owner can understand the request, authority, evidence, and next action without asking the caller to repeat unnecessary details.

## Methodology
Sample handoffs by intent and destination. Classify accepted, returned for clarification, misrouted, duplicate, and unresolved. Record exclusions and do not treat a transfer event as an accepted handoff.

## Key stats and takeaways
- Acceptance and transfer are different events.
- Minimum context should be defined per workflow.
- Sensitive or uncertain requests require explicit escalation ownership.

## Handoff design
Capture caller-stated intent, minimum required fields, action already taken, requested outcome, destination, owner, due point, and exception reason. Use neutral language and preserve uncertainty.

## Measurement table
| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acceptance | Destination confirms usable context | Did the owner receive enough evidence? |
| Return rate | Handoffs sent back for missing information | Which field is unclear? |
| Misroute rate | Handoffs reaching an unapproved destination | Is the routing tree current? |
| Exception age | Time unresolved after handoff | Who owns recovery? |

## FAQ
### Should the assistant summarize everything?
No. Send minimum necessary context plus the source record and clear next action.
### Who approves a new handoff field?
The workflow owner, with privacy and authority review when relevant.

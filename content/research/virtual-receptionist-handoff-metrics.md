## Headline finding

The BLS notes that customer-service work can include evenings, weekends, and holidays. Handoff metrics should therefore show whether coverage actually transfers work across schedules, not just how many calls were answered.

## Methodology

The scorecard follows a call from greeting to accepted next action. It separates agent-controlled quality from owner availability and records exceptions instead of hiding them in an average.

## Key stats and takeaways

- BLS projects about 341,700 annual openings for customer-service representatives.
- The core handoff measures are correct owner, complete context, acceptance, and closure.
- Average handle time is diagnostic, not the definition of a successful handoff.

## Scorecard

Track intent accuracy, required-field completeness, owner acceptance time, callback completion, and customer recontact. Review a sample of accepted and rejected handoffs. A rejection should have a reason code and updated owner, not a dead end.

Compare this with [backlog triage](/research/customer-call-backlog-triage) and [after-hours coverage](/research/after-hours-call-answering-benchmark). Scheduling teams can apply the same model to [booking metrics](/research/appointment-scheduling-call-center-metrics).

## FAQ

### What if the owner is unavailable?

Use a named backup and tell the caller the next action without promising an unmeasured time.

### Should every call create a ticket?

No. Create a durable record when a follow-up, exception, or compliance obligation exists.

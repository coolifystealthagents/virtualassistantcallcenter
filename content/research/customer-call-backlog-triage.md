## Headline finding

The BLS projects about 341,700 customer-service representative openings each year over 2024–2034. A backlog plan should therefore make work transferable: every item needs an intent, owner, next action, and age.

## Methodology

The model uses queue age, customer impact, and reversibility. It is a process recommendation, not a claim that one staffing ratio fits all teams.

## Key stats and takeaways

- BLS estimates 341,700 annual openings in the occupation.
- Queue age is useful only when timestamps are captured consistently.
- Triage should create a next action, not merely move a call to another list.

## Triage sequence

Mark each call urgent, time-sensitive, routine, or unclear. Assign a named owner and a due window. Merge duplicate requests only after confirming they refer to the same customer and issue. Close only when the customer-facing next step is complete.

The [missed-call recovery workflow](/blog/missed-call-recovery-workflow) provides a companion pattern for callbacks. Use [screening controls](/research/call-screening-workflow-for-small-business) when identity or payment-change requests are involved.

## Quality review

Review the oldest unresolved items first, then sample recently closed items for false closure. NIST's small-business guidance is a useful reminder to document ownership and recovery rather than relying on informal memory.

## FAQ

### Should the oldest call always go first?

No. Safety, outage, and deadline-sensitive issues can outrank age. Record the reason for the priority.

### Can one person own the whole queue?

Yes, if the owner has a backup and a defined escalation path.

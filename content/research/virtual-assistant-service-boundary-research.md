## Headline finding

Safe call assistance depends on a written boundary: what can be clarified and routed, what requires owner approval, and what must stop and escalate.

## Methodology

Map common caller intents to permitted action, required evidence, decision owner, fallback, and prohibited claim. Compare the map with privacy, accessibility, security, and consumer-protection guidance. Review ordinary, ambiguous, sensitive, and urgent examples without inferring a business-specific success rate.

## Key findings

- Clear limits improve escalation quality because the assistant can identify the missing authority.
- Minimum-necessary notes and understandable language are part of safe service, not optional polish.
- An assistant should not diagnose, decide emergencies, approve refunds, resolve identity disputes, or promise outcomes outside written authority.

## Evidence review

For each escalation, record the caller’s stated need, the boundary that applied, the evidence retained, the destination owner, and the unresolved next action. Owners should review changes to boundaries and exceptions. If guidance conflicts, pause and escalate rather than inventing a rule.

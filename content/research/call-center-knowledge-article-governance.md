## Headline finding
Knowledge is operational control only when an owner, source, review date, scope, and escalation path are visible. An unowned article is a risk signal, not a reliable answer.

## Methodology
Compare ten public sources with a local article register. Review a bounded sample for source traceability, freshness, audience, approved action, exception language, and owner. Separate content freshness from performance claims.

## Key stats and takeaways
- Five fields are foundational: owner, source, scope, review date, and escalation.
- Archive superseded guidance instead of silently overwriting it.
- Measure article use locally; sources do not create a universal deflection benchmark.

## Governance model
Use draft, approved, review, and retired states. Require an owner to approve changes that affect privacy, safety, authority, or customer promises. Put uncertainty in the article and route edge cases to a named manager.

## Measurement table
| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source traceability | Guidance links to an authoritative source | Can the claim be checked? |
| Freshness | Review date is within policy | Who reviews next? |
| Scope clarity | Audience and allowed action are explicit | Could a new agent misuse it? |
| Exception coverage | Edge cases have escalation instructions | Where does uncertainty go? |

## FAQ
### Can an agent edit approved knowledge?
Only through the documented review path.
### What happens when a source changes?
Flag the article, assign an owner, and update or retire it before relying on it.

## Headline finding
Every intake field should have a stated purpose, validation rule, owner, retention need, and safe failure state. A field is not justified merely because it is easy to ask for.

## Methodology
Inventory fields, map each to an approved workflow decision, and test complete, incomplete, ambiguous, and refused responses. Record which fields are required, optional, or prohibited. Do not calculate completion from records that bypassed the field.

## Key stats and takeaways
- Purpose and retention are as important as format validation.
- Refused and unknown values must remain visible.
- Accessibility alternatives should be tested with the same outcome definitions.

## Validation model
For each field specify allowed format, plain-language prompt, confirmation method, storage location, access role, retention period, and escalation. Keep payment credentials and unrelated sensitive information out of general notes.

## Measurement table
| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valid field rate | Values meet the published rule | Is the rule understandable? |
| Necessary field rate | Field supports a defined decision | Why is it collected? |
| Refusal visibility | Refused values are not treated as complete | Can an owner see the gap? |
| Repair rate | Records needing correction | Which prompt or tool fails? |

## FAQ
### Is more data better?
No. Collect the minimum necessary for the approved action.
### Who changes a required field?
The workflow owner, after privacy, security, and accessibility review where relevant.

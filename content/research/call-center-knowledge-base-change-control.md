## Headline finding

Six control stages keep call guidance trustworthy: propose, review, approve, publish, monitor, and retire. The stages are a workflow design, not a claim that every change has the same risk.

## Methodology

Compare ten public governance sources with a sample of knowledge edits. Define the source owner, review interval, effective date, affected workflow, rollback path, and stopping condition before measuring quality.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Six stages make ownership visible from proposal through retirement.
- Ten public sources can frame controls; local change records show whether they work.
- Unapproved wording should never become an agent’s default answer.

## Change table

| Stage | Required evidence | Owner question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Review | Source and impact | Is the instruction current? |
| Approval | Named authority | Who accepts the risk? |
| Retirement | Replacement or reason | What should the agent do now? |

## Operating controls

Give each article a version, owner, effective date, scope, and escalation note. Separate factual guidance from business promises. A virtual assistant can flag stale content and route proposed edits, but an owner approves legal, clinical, financial, safety, and pricing language.

## Quality review

Test a sample of high-use and recently changed guidance. Check that answers match the approved version, internal links resolve, and exceptions remain visible.

## FAQ

### Should every edit require the same review?

No. Use risk tiers, but document the tier and approver.

### What happens when sources conflict?

Pause the answer, record the conflict, and escalate to the designated owner.

### Is a last-edited date enough?

No. Retain the source, scope, approval, and effective date.

## Headline finding

Consistent routing means the same intent reaches the approved destination under the same documented rule, while legitimate location exceptions remain explicit and owned.

## Methodology

Build a routing matrix with intent, location signal, required information, destination, fallback, exception owner, and evidence. Compare a representative sample across locations and time periods. Separate routing agreement, handoff acceptance, and final resolution so one measure does not hide another.

## Key findings

- A shared rule needs version ownership and a process for local exceptions.
- Caller-provided location should be confirmed when it affects destination or eligibility.
- Unclear routing should be recorded as unresolved and escalated rather than silently assigned.

## Evidence review

Preserve the original intent, routing decision, rule version, destination, acceptance event, and correction history. Do not expose unnecessary location or identity details in a public-facing note. Owners approve legal, clinical, financial, safety, identity, and privacy exceptions.

## Headline finding

Four WCAG principles provide a useful test lens for phone workflows: perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust. An accommodation process must also name an owner and fallback.

## Methodology

Compare ten public sources with a sample of calls involving clarification, alternate channels, relay support, or repeat-back. Define the eligible population, accommodation evidence, exclusion rule, and stopping condition.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Four WCAG principles give a compact accessibility review lens.
- Ten sources can guide test design; local observations reveal actual barriers.
- A request for accommodation should never be treated as caller error.

## Review table

| Test | Evidence | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Language | Plain approved wording | Could the caller understand the next step? |
| Channel | Available alternative | Was preference honored? |
| Confirmation | Read-back or equivalent | Was intent captured accurately? |

## Operating controls

Offer approved alternatives, slow down when asked, and record only the minimum necessary preference. Route unresolved accommodation or legal questions to the designated owner.

## Quality review

Test different intents and channels. Check clarity, control operability, accurate read-back, privacy, access, and whether the accommodation outcome is evidenced.

## FAQ

### Is a transcript always the best alternative?

No. Use the owner-approved channel appropriate to the request and caller preference.

### Can the agent decide whether a request is reasonable?

No. It can capture and route the request; the owner decides.

### What if the caller cannot complete a verification step?

Apply the approved accessible fallback and escalate uncertainty.

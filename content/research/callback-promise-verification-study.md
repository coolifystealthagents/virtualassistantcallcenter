# Callback Promise Verification Study

Published October 13, 2026.

## Research question

Callback Promise Verification Study asks whether recorded callback promises reach an accepted owner and a destination-confirmed outcome within the declared window. The unit of analysis, observation window, eligible population, exclusions, and intended operational decision must be registered before outcomes are inspected. The study reports evidence for a bounded call workflow; it does not certify a vendor, infer caller intent, or establish a universal performance benchmark.

## Freeze the population and rule versions

In stage 1 of Callback Promise Verification Study, retain interaction ID, promise wording, caller timezone, promised window, assigned owner, acceptance, attempt, result, and verification. Link events with stable call and workflow identifiers rather than names or approximate timestamps. Preserve original timezones and source values, then add normalized fields separately. Report eligible, observed, excluded, unavailable, late, conflicting, and unresolved records as separate counts. This prevents a clean subset from silently representing the difficult calls most relevant to whether recorded callback promises reach an accepted owner and a destination-confirmed outcome within the declared window.

Stage 1 of Callback Promise Verification Study, freeze the population and rule versions, uses a predeclared classification guide and a second reviewer for a planned subset. Reviewers work independently before adjudication and record why competing classifications were rejected. Segment results by workflow version, queue, channel, shift, language or network condition only when that comparison was specified and sufficiently populated. An association may motivate a controlled test; it does not by itself prove that one factor caused the outcome.

Privacy at stage 1 of Callback Promise Verification Study requires pseudonymous study identifiers, minimum necessary fields, role-based access, a retention owner, and approved references to restricted recordings or transcripts. Do not copy customer profiles or credentials into an analysis file. Publish denominators and uncertainty, not worker rankings from small samples. Any operational response names its accountable owner, effective time, stop rule, rollback, and follow-up observation window.

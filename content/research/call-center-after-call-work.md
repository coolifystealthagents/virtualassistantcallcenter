## Headline finding

Measure note completeness and handoff quality alongside elapsed time. WCAG is organized around four principles, a useful reminder that a phone path should be perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust. That four-part accessibility lens is a design aid, not a universal performance benchmark.

## Methodology

This research note compares the ten public sources listed below with a practical call-operations control set. Separate external guidance from local measurements. For one representative review period, record the interaction timestamp, intent, route, action, handoff, exception, and final disposition. Exclude missing or ambiguous records from rate calculations and report the exclusion rather than filling it with an estimate.

## Key stats and takeaways

- The four WCAG principles provide a compact test lens for language, confirmation, alternatives, and escalation.
- A ten-source evidence set can support a control review, but it cannot establish a business-specific rate without local observations.
- Keep customer-facing wording narrower than internal goals; an owner must approve promises, exceptions, and regulated decisions.

## Operating model

Begin with a written intent definition. Name the allowed action, the minimum information required, the destination owner, and the fallback if the owner is unavailable. Create a separate “needs review” outcome so unresolved work is visible instead of being counted as completed.

For each interaction, preserve the source event, the action taken, and the next action. A virtual assistant can collect and normalize information, but the business owner retains authority over refunds, safety matters, legal requests, clinical decisions, identity disputes, and claims about outcomes. Do not publish public prices or rates in this workflow.

## Measurement table

| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent accuracy | Recorded intent matches the caller’s stated request | Can another reviewer reproduce the category? |
| Route completion | Approved destination accepted the handoff | Where did the work stop? |
| Evidence completeness | Required fields and source note are present | Can the owner audit the action? |
| Exception age | Time since an unresolved item was created | Who owns the next review? |

## Quality and safety controls

Review a documented sample for identity confirmation, minimum-necessary data, consent or preference handling, accurate read-back, accessibility, and escalation adherence. Do not collect payment-card data in a general note. Do not diagnose, make emergency decisions, or promise a callback, answer rate, conversion rate, or savings figure unless the owner has approved the exact language and can measure it.

When a request has scam, impersonation, unusual payment, privacy, or safety characteristics, record the evidence and escalate rather than guessing.

## FAQ

### Is this a universal benchmark?

No. It is a repeatable design and review method. Baseline the team’s own interactions, publish definitions, and revise targets only after clean observations.

### What should be done when the record is incomplete?

Mark it unresolved, retain only the minimum necessary details, and assign an owner for clarification. Do not silently infer a sensitive intent or outcome.

### Who approves exceptions?

The named business owner or delegated manager. The operating agent should pause and escalate when written rules do not cover the case.

## Headline finding

An attempted transfer is not an accepted handoff. Reliability analysis must show the trigger, destination, acceptance event, fallback, and accountable next action.

## Methodology

Define the handoff population, clock source, successful acceptance event, exclusion rules, and exception taxonomy before review. Compare ordinary, urgent, after-hours, and unreachable cases. Use local timestamps to assess performance; external sources can frame governance and accessibility questions but cannot supply a company benchmark.

## Key findings

- Separate technical connection from human acceptance.
- A fallback owner and visible unresolved state are necessary when the intended owner cannot accept.
- Accessibility and clear language are part of handoff reliability because a caller must understand what happens next.

## Evidence review

For each sample, preserve the original intent, approved destination, transfer attempt, acceptance or refusal, fallback route, and closure evidence. Escalate safety, privacy, identity, legal, clinical, and payment concerns under owner-approved rules. Do not promise a callback unless the owner has approved the promise and can control it.

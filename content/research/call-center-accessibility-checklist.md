## Headline finding

The W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines provide a shared accessibility reference for digital content. Phone operations should apply the same discipline to communication preferences, confirmations, and alternatives rather than treating accessibility as a website-only concern.

## Methodology

The checklist combines W3C principles with service-operations controls. It focuses on observable behavior: ask preference, adapt the interaction, confirm the next step, and record only what is needed.

## Key stats and takeaways

- WCAG is organized around perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust principles.
- An accessible handoff preserves the customer's chosen communication path.
- A backup channel should be offered, not silently substituted.

## Checklist

Ask whether the caller needs a communication accommodation. Use plain language, pause for confirmation, and offer written follow-up when appropriate. Confirm names, dates, and next steps in the requested format. Train staff on the difference between an accommodation request and a clinical or legal question.

Measure the result with [handoff metrics](/research/virtual-receptionist-handoff-metrics) and booking correction rates from [scheduling metrics](/research/appointment-scheduling-call-center-metrics).

## FAQ

### Should accessibility requests be treated as complaints?

No. Treat them as service requirements and route them to the owner who can fulfill them.

### Do scripts reduce accessibility?

Only when scripts prevent listening. Use scripts for safety and consistency, while allowing approved adaptations.

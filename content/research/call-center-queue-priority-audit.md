## Headline finding

Priority is defensible when the rule, evidence, owner, and review time are visible. An urgent label without those four elements can hide risk.

## Methodology

Compare ten public sources with a sample across ordinary, urgent, ambiguous, and duplicate calls. Define eligible population, priority rule, exclusion rule, evidence threshold, and stopping condition before scoring.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Four elements make priority reviewable: rule, evidence, owner, and review time.
- Ten sources can inform control design; local records establish queue behavior.
- Ambiguous urgency belongs in an exception path, not a guessed category.

## Audit table

| Element | Evidence | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rule | Versioned criterion | Is the priority reproducible? |
| Owner | Accountable person or queue | Who acts next? |
| Review time | Recorded review event | Was the exception seen? |

## Operating controls

Use plain categories, collect minimum details, and route safety, legal, clinical, privacy, or financial requests to approved owners. Never promise priority or response time without local measurement.

## Quality review

Sample each category and age band. Check accurate read-back, source evidence, accessibility, access control, duplicate handling, and disposition quality.

## FAQ

### Is the caller’s wording enough to set priority?

It is evidence to assess, not a substitute for the owner-approved rule.

### What if a priority rule is missing?

Use the documented uncertainty path and escalate.

### How often should priorities be recalculated?

At an owner-defined cadence tied to risk and queue change.

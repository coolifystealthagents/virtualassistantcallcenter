## Headline finding

WHO's consolidated telemedicine guidance calls out governance, workforce, infrastructure, policy, and compliance as parts of service design. A phone intake checklist should make those boundaries visible rather than asking a scheduler to improvise.

## Methodology

This is an operational checklist, not clinical or legal advice. It distinguishes scheduling facts from clinical questions and uses HHS HIPAA material as the privacy reference for covered entities.

## Key stats and takeaways

- WHO's digital-health strategy was adopted in 2020.
- Scheduling staff should collect only fields needed for the next approved action.
- Clinical advice belongs with qualified clinical staff and a documented escalation route.

## Minimum intake

Capture patient name, safe callback method, appointment purpose in the patient's own words, preferred location or modality, availability, and any scheduling prerequisite stated by the practice. Confirm the final slot and next step aloud.

Use the [scheduling metrics framework](/research/appointment-scheduling-call-center-metrics) to audit correction rates. For suspicious requests or identity uncertainty, use the [screening workflow](/research/call-screening-workflow-for-small-business).

## Privacy and escalation

Avoid recording diagnoses or payment details when they are not required. HHS privacy and security guidance should be reviewed with the practice's compliance owner. If a caller describes an emergency, follow the practice's emergency script and local policy.

## FAQ

### Can a scheduler decide urgency?

Only within the practice's approved script. Otherwise the call should be escalated.

### What is the best audit sample?

Include every shift and common appointment type, with extra review after any privacy or booking error.

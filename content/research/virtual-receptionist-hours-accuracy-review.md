## Headline finding

Published hours are reliable only when the public statement, local schedule, exception dates, caller-facing message, and correction owner agree. A correct web page cannot compensate for a contradictory phone message.

## Research question

This review asks whether a caller receives an accurate route for the time and location they selected. Separate normal hours, holiday exceptions, temporary closures, time-zone ambiguity, and after-hours fallback. Treat a stale message as a distinct finding from a wrong underlying schedule.

## Methodology

Create a dated sample of public hours, internal approved schedules, phone messages, and route outcomes. Include at least one ordinary period and each relevant exception period. Record location, time zone, source version, test outcome, and owner. Do not infer hours from a caller's number or from a neighboring location. Report unavailable evidence.

## Measurement table

| Measure | Question | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement agreement | Do public channels match? | Approved schedule and page |
| Route agreement | Does the call follow the right path? | Call outcome and destination |
| Exception freshness | Was the change reflected? | Version and update record |

## Findings

Hours accuracy is a cross-channel control. A schedule can be correct while a recorded message still gives the old route. Location pages can also disagree when one exception applies to a single site. Review the exact wording, not only the opening and closing times. If an urgent option exists, confirm that the message explains its limits without promising that an owner is available.

## Accessibility and ownership

Use understandable language and offer the approved alternative when the normal path is closed. W3C guidance supports clear, operable communication. A support specialist can state the published hours and capture a correction request, but an owner should approve changes to the schedule. Keep the correction record separate from the public article and do not collect unnecessary personal information.

## Limitations

A test at one moment cannot prove that hours remain accurate throughout a season. A public statement may be correct while a caller experiences a route failure elsewhere. A small sample cannot estimate all exception risk. State the test period, locations, channels, and missing evidence.

## Conclusion

Hours reviews should follow the caller across statement, route, exception, and correction ownership. That approach finds stale messages and local mismatches that a single page check misses.

## Additional analysis

Hours accuracy should be tested as a calendar and communication problem. A location can open at the approved time while a caller hears a closed message. A public page can show the correct holiday exception while a directory or voicemail uses the ordinary schedule. Record each channel, its source version, the time zone shown, and the owner responsible for corrections. A test that checks only the website measures page accuracy, not caller access.

Define a test calendar before opening the sources. Include ordinary weekdays, weekends where relevant, holidays, temporary closures, and a boundary minute immediately before or after a change. The exact test time matters because a route may change at midnight in one location but at a central system's time zone in another. Preserve the test result and source date. Do not infer a missing exception from a neighboring location.

Review the message a caller receives after hours. It should say what the caller can do now, what information is safe to leave, and how approved urgent exceptions are handled. It should not imply that an owner is monitoring a route unless that is true for the tested period. If a caller asks for an accessibility accommodation or another channel, the response should identify the approved option or an accountable fallback.

Temporary changes need a correction path. Identify who may approve the change, who updates each public channel, and how the business checks that the update propagated. If one channel cannot be changed immediately, the message should not conceal that limitation. Measure the time between approval and channel correction only when both events have reliable timestamps. Otherwise, report the gap qualitatively.

For multi-location services, calculate agreement by location before producing an aggregate. A small location with one stale route should remain visible even if the central result looks accurate. Separate policy differences from errors. Local hours may legitimately differ, but each difference should have a source, owner, and caller-facing explanation. A consistent test method can coexist with local schedules.

## Conclusion

Hours accuracy is a cross-channel, time-zone, and ownership measure. It becomes useful when the research records the statement, route, exception, correction, and local context that a caller actually encounters.

Report the number of channels tested and the number of exceptions in each. A single stale message matters operationally, but it should not be converted into a universal error percentage without a defined population of messages and locations. A qualitative finding may be the right result when the business has only a few published routes. The reader should know whether the review was a complete inventory, a sample, or a spot check.

When a public statement is corrected, repeat the same test after the approved change. Record the correction time and the next observation time only if both are reliable. A successful retest shows that the route matched at that moment; it does not prove that future holiday changes will propagate. The recurring control belongs to the business owner, who should define the review cadence and evidence retained.
The test should include the caller's likely path, including the greeting, receptionist explanation, transfer, and fallback message. If any step uses a different time-zone assumption, record it. Report channels tested and exceptions in each. A stale message matters operationally, but it should not become a universal error percentage without a defined population of messages and locations.

A correction review should examine whether the new statement reached every relevant channel. If a phone greeting, directory, local page, and central page are controlled by different owners, record the handoff between them. The absence of an update timestamp is itself a limitation. Do not backfill one from a file date or Git timestamp. The business should define which source is authoritative and how a caller can report a contradiction.

If the public route includes a transfer, test both acceptance and failure. A caller may hear correct hours and still reach an unavailable destination. Record the fallback message, owner, and unresolved state. Those observations belong in the hours review because a schedule is useful only when it leads to an understandable, accountable next step.

A useful correction record identifies the approved source, the observed contradiction, the owner notified, and the next verification time. It should not include a caller's unnecessary personal details. If the contradiction affects urgent or accessibility-related routing, flag that risk under the approved escalation rule. This keeps the review tied to caller safety and clarity rather than treating hours as a static web-page field.

The review should preserve the exact public wording that caused confusion, while removing any personal caller information from the published study.

Retain the test period, source version, location, and owner with the result. Repeat the same route check after an approved correction and record whether the contradiction has cleared across all relevant channels and operating periods for callers.

## FAQ
### Should every exception be published?
Publish the information callers need, under the business's approved correction process.
### Who approves a schedule change?
The designated business owner, not an intake specialist acting alone.

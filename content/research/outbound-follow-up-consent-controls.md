## Headline finding

The FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule is a reminder that outbound follow-up needs a permission and suppression design. A callback workflow should make “do not contact” a durable state, not a note that can be lost.

## Methodology

This article maps FTC and FCC guidance to a simple operational control set. It does not interpret a business's legal obligations; legal counsel should review channel, jurisdiction, and consent specifics.

## Key stats and takeaways

- The National Do Not Call Registry began in 2003, according to the FTC.
- Consent, channel, purpose, and stop requests should be recorded separately.
- A human should review ambiguous or high-risk contact records before outreach.

## Control sequence

Store source, timestamp, channel, stated purpose, and contact preference. Check the suppression list before each attempt. Use a concise identity and purpose statement. Process a stop request immediately, then preserve the suppression event.

Pair this with [call screening](/research/call-screening-workflow-for-small-business) for inbound replies and [backlog triage](/research/customer-call-backlog-triage) for unresolved prospects.

## Measurement

Measure eligible records, suppressed records, attempted contacts, stop requests, wrong-party outcomes, and completed handoffs. Do not optimize only for conversations; a low complaint rate and clean suppression behavior matter more.

## FAQ

### Can a lead request a callback by phone?

Yes, but record what was requested and the permitted channel. Do not infer broad permission from a narrow request.

### Should an assistant keep calling after a stop request?

No. Stop processing and follow the business's suppression procedure.

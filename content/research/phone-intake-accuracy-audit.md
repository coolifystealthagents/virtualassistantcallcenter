# Auditing phone intake accuracy against the source record

*October 22, 2026*

Phone intake accuracy is the agreement between what the caller provided and what the agent recorded. It is different from customer satisfaction and from call quality, and it can be audited without listening to every call.

## Define the fields

Choose the fields that drive the next action: name, callback number, reference, issue category, address, and consent or preference flags.

For each field, write the acceptable source: the caller's statement, the account record, or a system lookup. An agent who copies an address from the account record should not be scored as if the caller dictated it.

## Sample and record

Sample calls across queues, shifts, and tenure. For each, listen to the relevant segment and compare it with the note and the downstream record.

Record correct, incorrect, missing, and not applicable. Preserve the error type: misheard name, wrong number, transposed digit, wrong category, or omitted consent.

## Weight by consequence

A wrong callback number is a service failure; a wrong consent flag is a compliance issue. Report the errors by class and by consequence rather than a single accuracy percentage.

## Check the downstream effect

Where possible, follow the record to the next step. Did the customer have to call back, did the appointment fail, did the transfer restart? The downstream effect turns an accuracy score into an operational finding.

## Report and improve

Share the sample size, the field-level accuracy, and the top error classes. Fix the script or the system field before coaching individual agents.

If one field is consistently wrong, the cause is often the form or the audio quality, not the person.

## Limitations

Listening to sampled calls is time-consuming, and the sample may miss rare but serious errors. The method measures recorded accuracy, not whether the caller understood the interaction.

## Headline finding
After-hours coverage is a decision system, not a blanket promise. It must state what can be handled, what is deferred, who owns urgent exceptions, and how the fallback is recorded.

## Methodology
Map the published coverage window to ten public sources and local call records. Test ordinary, urgent, inaccessible, ambiguous, and no-owner scenarios. Report observed counts only after defining eligibility and exclusions.

## Key stats and takeaways
- Every covered path needs a fallback owner.
- Urgent does not mean the assistant decides what is medically, legally, or physically urgent.
- Coverage claims require local measurement and owner approval.

## Operating model
Publish a narrow scope, collect minimum context, provide approved next steps, and mark deferred work visibly. For safety or emergency language, use the owner-approved escalation instruction. Do not invent response times.

## Measurement table
| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage eligibility | Calls within the documented scope | Is the boundary clear? |
| Fallback readiness | Named owner for unavailable paths | Can work move after hours? |
| Escalation evidence | Sensitive cases retain reason and owner | Is the decision auditable? |
| Deferred visibility | Deferred records have next action | Is backlog visible? |

## FAQ
### Can after-hours coverage promise a callback?
Only if the owner has approved and can measure the exact promise.
### What if no owner responds?
Record the exception and follow the approved safety or business fallback; do not claim resolution.

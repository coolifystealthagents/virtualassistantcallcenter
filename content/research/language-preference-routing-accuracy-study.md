## Study aim and eligible calls

The study asks whether a structured, caller-stated language preference improves routing to available support. Include calls where the caller requests another available language or where staff explicitly ask and receive a preference. Do not infer a preference from accent, name, or location for eligibility.

Compare a baseline using ordinary free-text notes with a prospective period using defined fields and staff guidance. The study should evaluate communication workflow, not classify caller identity or language ability.

## Measures and privacy controls

The primary outcome is correct first routing according to the caller's stated preference and available service. Secondary measures include repeated preference explanations, transfers, callback completion, and correction requests. Record denominators and distinguish unavailable service from an assistant's routing error.

Use coded operational fields and avoid retaining call audio or transcript excerpts when they are not needed. Restrict access, define deletion dates for analysis extracts, and allow preference corrections to flow into the study data.

## Analysis plan

Compare routing accuracy before and after implementation with confidence intervals when volume permits. Stratify by requested language and shift only when subgroup sizes protect privacy and support credible interpretation. Review a small sample of failures to identify whether the cause was capture, availability, routing, or stale profile data.

Test sensitivity by counting unknown outcomes as both correct and incorrect. Report service unavailability separately so an apparent workflow improvement does not conceal limited access.

## Interpretation boundary

Adopt the structured field if accuracy improves beyond the preset threshold without more abandoned calls, privacy complaints, or stale preferences. Findings remain specific to the languages, staffing, and period observed. They cannot support claims about a person's background, proficiency, or future preference.

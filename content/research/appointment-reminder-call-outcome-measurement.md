## Headline finding

Reminder contact and appointment attendance are different outcomes. A reliable study records the attempt, the caller’s confirmed intent, and the owner’s final disposition separately.

## Methodology

Define the eligible appointment population, reminder window, duplicate rule, exclusion rule, and stopping condition. Compare ten public sources with local records and report unknown outcomes rather than treating them as confirmations.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Three outcome states prevent contact from being mistaken for attendance.
- Ten sources can define controls, but local records are needed for a baseline.
- Unknown, declined, and unreachable cases need explicit dispositions.

## Measurement table

| Event | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attempt | Approved reminder action | Was the channel authorized? |
| Confirmation | Caller’s stated intent | Was it read back accurately? |
| Outcome | Owner-defined final state | Is attendance actually known? |

## Operating controls

Use clear wording, collect the minimum necessary information, and route cancellation or clinical questions to the approved owner. Do not diagnose, disclose sensitive details broadly, or promise an outcome.

## Quality review

Review successful, declined, unreachable, and escalated calls. Check identity handling, consent or preference records, accessibility, and whether the final state has evidence.

## FAQ

### Does a voicemail count as confirmation?

No, unless the owner has defined that state explicitly.

### Can reminder performance be compared monthly?

Only when populations, clocks, exclusions, and outcome definitions remain stable.

### Who handles a medical question?

The designated clinical or business owner, never an unapproved operating agent.

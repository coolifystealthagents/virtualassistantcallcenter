## Research question

When callers decline an optional intake question, does the assistant continue with an appropriate service path, and what outcome follows? The study should not treat refusal itself as an error. A caller may reasonably avoid sharing information that appears unnecessary, sensitive, or poorly explained.

The [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework) describes privacy risk management, including communication and data processing. It supports examining whether collection has a defined purpose. It does not decide which fields a particular business may require.

## Define required and optional fields

Before sampling calls, obtain the field list and policy version that applied to each interaction. Mark which questions were required for the requested service, optional, conditional, or prohibited. If management cannot supply that classification, record a governance gap rather than asking reviewers to invent one.

Sample calls containing a refusal, hesitation, or direct question about why information is needed. Code the field, assistant explanation, whether pressure or repeated asking occurred, whether an alternative path was offered, and the eventual handoff or abandonment outcome. Remove unrelated personal content from the study dataset.

## Examine the response

Review whether the assistant accurately explained the purpose, respected an optional refusal, and escalated a required-field conflict through the written path. Separate calls where the caller disconnected immediately from calls that ended after repeated questioning. A temporal sequence can suggest where friction occurred, but it does not prove why the caller left.

The [FTC's business guidance on collecting and protecting personal information](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security) offers general data-security principles. It does not provide a call script or determine whether a field is operationally necessary.

## Add caller perspective carefully

If the business has approval to conduct follow-up research, invite a small, separate sample to describe whether the question's purpose was clear and whether they felt able to decline. Participation should not affect service. Do not contact people who opted out of follow-up or use an operational callback as disguised research recruitment.

Compare these accounts with the call coding. Differences are useful: a script may look compliant on paper while callers find the explanation confusing.

## Limitations

Refusal calls are not representative of all callers. People who agree to a follow-up interview may differ from those who do not. Reviewers can mishear hesitation, and transcripts may miss tone. Service outcomes depend on staffing, eligibility, and queue conditions as well as the question.

This design can show how one operation handles documented refusals and where its script creates friction. It cannot measure population-wide privacy preferences, establish legal compliance, or prove that a particular question caused abandonment.

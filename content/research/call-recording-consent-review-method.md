## Headline finding

Recording review has four distinct states: notice given, preference captured, recording retained, and access authorized. Collapsing them into one checkbox obscures the control.

## Methodology

Compare ten public privacy, security, and accessibility sources with a representative recording sample. Define notice wording, refusal path, retention rule, access owner, exclusion rule, and stopping condition.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Four states separate communication, choice, storage, and access.
- Ten sources can inform a review design; local records establish compliance evidence.
- A refusal or uncertain state should route to a documented alternative.

## Review table

| State | Evidence | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Notice | Approved wording and timestamp | Was notice understandable? |
| Preference | Caller choice | Was the choice honored? |
| Access | Authorized reviewer | Why was the recording opened? |

## Operating controls

Use owner-approved notices and alternatives. Retain only what the business requires, restrict access, and route legal or jurisdiction-specific questions to counsel or the business owner. Do not treat a recording as permission for unrelated use.

## Quality review

Review accepted, declined, interrupted, and disputed examples. Check notice, preference, access, retention, accessibility, and minimum-necessary handling.

## FAQ

### Is one notice valid everywhere?

Do not assume that. The owner must approve wording and routing for applicable jurisdictions.

### What if the caller declines?

Apply the approved non-recorded path and record only the minimum operational state.

### Who reviews disputes?

The designated privacy, legal, or business owner.

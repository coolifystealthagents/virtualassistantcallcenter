## Headline finding

After-hours coverage is reviewable when callers receive clear options, urgent cases have an approved exception path, and every unresolved request has a named owner.

## Methodology

Map the published after-hours path, message wording, accessibility alternatives, time-zone assumptions, urgent-case rule, fallback destination, and owner. Test ordinary, ambiguous, urgent, inaccessible, and abandoned interactions separately. External accessibility guidance supplies a review lens; local observations establish whether the path works.

## Key findings

- A message should distinguish information, ordinary follow-up, and approved urgent escalation.
- “Leave a message” is not an ownership model unless receipt, review, and exception handling are defined.
- Do not use an after-hours script to diagnose, make emergency decisions, or promise an unmonitored response.

## Review questions

Can a caller understand the available route, the expected next step, and what to do when the route fails? Can the owner identify every unresolved request and its age? Record the answer and the evidence source; do not convert a failed attempt into coverage.

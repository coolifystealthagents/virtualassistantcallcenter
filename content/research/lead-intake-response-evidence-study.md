## Headline finding

Lead response should be measured from an accepted intake event to a documented next action, not from an unverified marketing timestamp.

## Methodology

Define the eligible lead population, intake clock, duplicate rule, consent or preference record, exclusion rule, and stopping condition. Compare ten public sources with local records and report missing events separately.

## Key stats and takeaways

- Two events anchor the study: accepted intake and documented next action.
- Ten sources can guide safeguards; local records establish response evidence.
- A lead is not a promise, conversion, or customer outcome.

## Measurement table

| Measure | Definition | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted intake | Owner-defined valid request | Is the population eligible? |
| Next action | Approved follow-up event | Who accepted responsibility? |
| Exception | Missing, declined, or disputed state | Is recovery assigned? |

## Operating controls

Capture consent or channel preference where required, use minimum-necessary notes, and route pricing, legal, financial, or regulated questions to the owner. Do not publish public rates or imply a guaranteed response.

## Quality review

Sample new, duplicate, declined, unreachable, and escalated leads. Check source evidence, route accuracy, read-back, access, and disposition completeness.

## FAQ

### Does an auto-reply count as owner action?

Only if the owner has defined that event as the relevant next action.

### Can the team publish a response-time promise?

Only after local measurement and owner approval of the exact wording.

### What if consent is unclear?

Pause outreach and escalate to the owner’s privacy process.

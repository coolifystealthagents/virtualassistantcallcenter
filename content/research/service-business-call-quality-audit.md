## Headline finding

BLS describes customer-service work as including problem resolution, recordkeeping, and busy periods outside ordinary hours. A quality audit should test those behaviors directly rather than rewarding short calls.

## Methodology

Use a stratified sample by shift, intent, agent, and outcome. Score observable evidence: correct identification, useful notes, accurate answer or handoff, privacy-safe behavior, and clear next action.

## Key stats and takeaways

- BLS projects a 5 percent decline in customer-service representative employment from 2024 to 2034 while still projecting about 341,700 annual openings.
- A small, consistent sample is more useful than a large inconsistent scorecard.
- Every failed item needs a coaching or process action.

## Audit form

Record call date, shift, intent, disposition, and whether the next owner accepted the work. Mark evidence as pass, needs review, or fail. Separate agent error from missing policy or unavailable owner. Recheck corrected calls so the audit measures learning.

Use [handoff metrics](/research/virtual-receptionist-handoff-metrics) for acceptance and [privacy controls](/research/remote-call-center-privacy-controls) for data handling.

## FAQ

### Should audits be announced?

The quality program should be documented and transparent. Local recording and employment rules may affect implementation.

### What is a passing score?

Set thresholds by risk. A privacy failure should not be averaged away by several easy calls.
